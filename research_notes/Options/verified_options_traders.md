# Verified options track records: who has one, and what blew up

Web search, 27 Sept 2026. Mostly news and regulator sources; figures as reported there.

## How to judge a track record
- Only audited fund statements or real-money contests count. Broker screenshots and online "verified" services are easy
  to fake and suffer survivorship bias (people only post winners).
  [CV5 Capital](https://www.cv5capital.io/insights/the-importance-of-audited-track-records-for-hedge-funds)
- The U.S. Investing Championship verifies real-money accounts; options are allowed in its "enhanced growth" division.
  Winners post huge one-year returns (e.g. +90.7% in 2024 at $1M+), but one year of a contest says little about repeatability.
  [Business Wire 2024 results](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250127956815/en/2024-United-States-Investing-Championship-Final-Results),
  [USIC overview](https://trading-tournaments.com/tournaments/championships/usic)

## The most verified "option traders": rule-based indexes
- Cboe BXM (covered calls on the S&P 500) and PUT (put-writing) have public, rules-based histories since 1986. They show
  lower volatility and smaller worst drawdowns than the S&P 500, with better risk-adjusted returns in Cboe-sponsored studies,
  but they still fall hard in crashes and lag in strong bull markets.
  [Cboe BXM factsheet](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/indices/factsheet/CboeGlobalIndices_BXM-Index.pdf),
  [Wilshire study for Cboe](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/spx/wilshire-options-based-benchmark-indexes-2019.pdf)

## Verified winner, with a catch
- Universa (Spitznagel, advised by Taleb) reported +3,612% in March 2020 and +4,144% year to date, from tail-risk put
  hedging. That return is on the small hedge sleeve, not a whole portfolio; the strategy loses a little most years and pays
  off in crashes. [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-04-08/taleb-advised-universa-tail-risk-fund-returned-3-600-in-march),
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universa_Investments)

## Famous option sellers who blew up (all sold options with undefined risk)
- LJM Preservation & Growth: sold naked S&P puts; lost ~80% in two days in Feb 2018 ("Volmageddon"), $812M to $14M;
  SEC/CFTC charges followed. [Yahoo/Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/u-ljm-fund-lost-most-152828038.html),
  [GARP](https://www.garp.org/risk-intelligence/market/ljm-fund-managers-face-cftc-sec-charges-chief-risk-officer-settles)
- OptionSellers.com (James Cordier): naked natural-gas calls; ~$150M lost in Nov 2018, many clients lost 100% and owed
  margin debt. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2018/11/21/a-risky-natural-gas-bet-gone-awry-leads-to-weepy-youtube-confessional.html)
- Malachite Capital: ex-Goldman, ~$600M, sold crash protection with leverage; shut in March 2020, Credit Suisse lost $214M.
  [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-03-17/malachite-capital-to-wind-down-funds-amid-market-volatility)
- "Karen the Supertrader" (Karen Bruton): celebrated option seller on a TV network; the SEC found she hid >$50M of losses
  by rolling options to show steady monthly gains. [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/litreleases/2021/lr25246.htm)

## Lessons for our options book
1. Steady monthly option-selling income is exactly what blow-ups look like until the crash: never sell uncapped risk.
2. Defined-risk only (spreads, bought options), with max loss known before entry and capped per trade and in total.
3. Mark every position to market daily; rolling a loser forward must not hide it (the Karen lesson).
4. Judge the book only on the full record, never on a good month; use the Cboe indexes as the honest benchmark.
5. Small tail protection can be valuable but costs money most years; size it as insurance, not a profit engine.
