# Options YouTubers: who teaches, who sells

Checked 28 September 2026 using web searches, public court and regulator records, and the creators' own sites. Many YouTube pages would not load for automated reading, so some video titles and prices come from search snippets or third-party reviews. Those are marked below. The ratings are our own judgement. They are **not** findings of wrongdoing. Where the table mentions a regulator or court, it reports only what that body actually said or ordered.

## 1. Bottom line

**None of the 12 creators we checked has an audited, multi-year options track record that includes the losing years.** Warrior Trading posts accountant-reviewed statements, but it teaches stock day trading, not options, and we could not confirm how far that review went. The honest channels are useful free textbooks. They teach how options work (Greeks, implied volatility, spreads, position sizing), they talk openly about losses, and they don't promise income. The best of these are **tastylive, projectfinance (Chris Butler) and Option Alpha**. All three are still paid by brokers when you open accounts or trade more.

The further a channel moves toward "$X a month", lifestyle clips, paid alerts or a Discord, and courses costing $1,000 to $30,000, the weaker its evidence gets. Two businesses in this group, RagingBull and Warrior Trading, settled with the FTC over earnings claims. The FTC complaint against RagingBull alleged that its two leaders lost money on their own trades. So: use the good channels to learn how options work. Don't take anyone's income claims, trade alerts or "copy my portfolio" link as proof that a strategy makes money. For that, look at the market evidence in section 4.

## 3. Red flags checklist (use on any trading influencer)

Count the ticks. One or two is normal for a business. Five or more means you are watching an ad, not a lesson.

- [ ] **Win-rate or P&L screenshots** instead of full broker statements over several years. Screenshots are easy to fake and easy to cherry-pick.
- [ ] **No losing trades or losing years shown.** Ask what they did in 2020, 2022 and Feb 2018.
- [ ] **"90% win rate".** Selling options wins most of the time by design. Win rate says nothing about profit. One loss can erase 10 to 20 wins.
- [ ] **"$X a month / weekly income / passive / safe"** from selling premium. That income is mostly how much capital was put at risk in a calm market.
- [ ] **Paid Discord, alerts, or "copy my trades".** The seller is paid for signups, not for your results.
- [ ] **Broker affiliate or partner links.** The more you trade, the more someone earns.
- [ ] **Prices revealed only on a sales call**, prices that depend on your account size, or no refunds.
- [ ] **0DTE / "lottery" trades** or "turn $500 into $50k" titles.
- [ ] **Urgency**: "last chance", "market is about to crash/rip", countdown timers.
- [ ] **Lifestyle**: cars, travel, mansions, "quit your job".
- [ ] **Vague credentials** ("ex-Wall Street", "ex-Goldman") with no firm, title or dates.
- [ ] **Only testimonials**, no data, or reviews that come mostly from affiliate sites.
- [ ] **"Realized" profit only.** Wheel and rolling strategies can hide losses in stock you were assigned or in trades you rolled forward. "Karen the Supertrader" hid more than $50M of losses by rolling (SEC).
- [ ] **Backtests sold as results.** A backtest is a hypothesis. Live fills and costs are worse.
- [ ] **Sues or threatens critics** instead of publishing statements.
- [ ] **A regulator action on record.** Check FINRA BrokerCheck, SEC IAPD, SEC and FTC press releases, and your state securities regulator.

**Green flags:** full statements including losses, trades logged before they are made, costs and slippage shown, "most traders lose money" said out loud, no need to buy anything to learn the basics.

## 4. What the real market evidence says

The material below is from `reports/Options rulebook.md` and `reports/AI options trading research.md`.

- **There is one proven edge, and it is small.** Index option sellers get paid on average because they are insuring others against crashes. The VIX averaged 19.3% from 1990 to 2018, while the S&P 500 then actually moved only 15.1%. That gap arrives as **many small wins and a few big losses**.
- **The best public, rules-based option records are the Cboe indexes**, not any YouTuber. Since 2007, PUT (put-writing on the S&P 500) returned 7.1% a year against 11.0% for the S&P 500. It had less risk, but equal or lower risk-adjusted return and clearly lower total return. In 2020 PUT made +2.1% while the S&P 500 made +18.4%. Its worst month was −17.65% (Oct 2008).
- **Covered calls are mostly just owning the stock.** About two-thirds of the risk is stock risk, and the part that pays is under 10% of it (AQR). **Protective puts cost about 1.8% a year** in lost return.
- **Retail option traders lose mostly to trading costs.** Short-dated options have quoted spreads of about 12.6%. Retail traders lost about **$241k a day in SPX 0DTE options** (2021–23), mostly to costs and mostly on buying single options.
- **Our own 10-year replay** of a small monthly SPY put spread, the "safe income" trade these channels teach, came out about **zero at a 2%-wide spread and slightly negative (−0.076R per trade) at the $2 width we can afford**, even with win rates of 66–84%. Costs ate 13% to 81% of the credit.
- **The famous option-selling "income" funds blew up**: LJM lost about 80% in two days (Feb 2018), OptionSellers.com lost about $150M in 2018, Malachite shut down in 2020, and Karen the Supertrader hid losses (SEC). All of them sold risk with no cap.
- **No AI tool or retail options product publishes audited live profits.** AI trading contests with real money mostly lost.
- **Honest expectations:** with small defined-risk spreads, expect no edge at first. Two weeks of profit and loss proves nothing, and you need about 50 to 100 closed trades before the numbers mean anything. Paper fills look better than real ones. Always compare against simply holding SPY.

**So the real market looks like this:** small, slow, cost-sensitive, with rare large losses. The social-media version shows frequent wins, monthly income, a screenshot and a link to buy. Our rulebook follows the real market: paper only, SPY put spreads only, defined risk, about $250 of worst-case risk per trade, and no 0DTE, naked selling or rolling.
