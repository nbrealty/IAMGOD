# Trade slow trends, buy dips, size small

An autonomous Claude paper-trading agent on a small retail account should run a short list of simple, published, codeable rules, not invent strategies. The evidence supports four: a monthly trend-filtered asset-allocation core (Faber's 10-month SMA rule, optionally blended with Antonacci's dual momentum), a daily RSI(2) buy-the-dip sleeve on index ETFs, a small regime-gated momentum/breakout sleeve in liquid stocks, and an optional long/flat crypto trend sleeve. All positions are sized by volatility and a fixed 0.5% risk per trade, under hard drawdown breakers enforced by code. Every classic edge has decayed. Published anomalies earn about **58% less after publication** ([McLean & Pontiff](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12365)). Faber's timing model roughly **halved its CAGR out of sample, from 11.7% to about 6%**, though it kept a low drawdown ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.substack.com/p/global-tactical-asset-allocation)). The honest target is therefore equity-like returns of about 6–10% a year with shallower drawdowns than buy-and-hold, not outsized gains. On €5,000 that is €300–500 a year before tax. Retail-outcome studies set the stakes. **97% of Brazilians who day-traded futures for more than 300 days lost money** ([Chague et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)), **74–89% of EU retail CFD accounts lose** ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-agrees-prohibit-binary-options-and-restrict-cfds-protect-retail-investors)), and the most active US traders trailed the market by 6.5 points a year ([Barber & Odean](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=219228)). The agent's first job is to avoid the behaviours that produced those numbers: high turnover, leverage, discretionary overrides and oversized bets. Its second job is to beat a buy-and-hold index ETF after costs and taxes. If it cannot, the index ETF is the answer.

## Retail traders mostly lose, so 6–10% a year is the realistic bar

The retail evidence is consistent across three continents, and its lesson is about activity, not intelligence. In **Brazil**, Chague, De-Losso and Giovannetti followed everyone who started day trading mini-index futures in 2013–2015. Of the 1,551 who persisted more than 300 days, **97% lost money, only 1.1% earned more than the minimum wage**, and there was no evidence of learning with experience ([SSRN 3423101](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)). An FGV EESP study of CVM data found that individuals lost **R$9.9 billion** day trading between March 2020 and end-2023, about R$10.2k per person before fees and courses. Individuals as a group were negative on **more than 95% of days** ([FGV EESP](https://portal.fgv.br/sites/default/files/uploads/eesp-pesquisa-day-trade-e-pandemia.pdf)). A 2025 broker-sample survey of 33,995 day traders still found **71.87% losing** over twelve months ([Monitor Mercantil](https://monitormercantil.com.br/day-trade-causa-perdas-para-7187-dos-investidores-pessoas-fisicas/)). In the **EU**, national regulators found that 74–89% of retail CFD accounts lose, with average losses of €1,600–29,000 per client. That finding led ESMA to ban binary options and cap CFD leverage, and brokers must still print the loss percentage on every page ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-agrees-prohibit-binary-options-and-restrict-cfds-protect-retail-investors)). The same regime applies to Portuguese investors. In the **US**, Barber and Odean's 66,465 discount-broker households show the cost of turnover. The most active quintile earned **11.4% a year against the market's 17.9%** ([Barber & Odean](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=219228)). More than 80% of Taiwanese day traders lost money in a typical six-month period ([Barber et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386418113000190)). Retail options buyers lost about **$2.1 billion** between late 2019 and mid-2021 ([Bryzgalova, Pavlova & Sikorskaya](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827/)). Professionals do little better against a passive benchmark: **79% of active US large-cap funds lagged the S&P 500 in 2025** ([SPIVA](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/spiva/spiva-us-year-end-2025.pdf)), and about 89.5% lagged it over 15 years ([Ritholtz on SPIVA](https://ritholtz.com/2025/05/the-data-on-active-large-cap-underperformance/)).

These studies say that losses scale with frequency, leverage and option buying. The agent's design follows directly: swing and position timeframes, liquid ETFs and large caps, and no CFDs, 0DTE options or mini-index scalping. The Brazilian finding of no learning also means the agent cannot assume it will improve with practice. Only statistically tested evidence should change its behaviour.

The return ceiling comes from market history and factor decay. Global equities returned about **6.6% a year after inflation over 1900–2025** ([UBS Yearbook 2026](https://www.ubs.com/global/en/media/display-page-ndp/en-20260303-global-investment-returns-yearbook-2026.html)). Across 97 published predictors, returns were **26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication** ([McLean & Pontiff](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12365)). A workable planning rule follows: halve any backtested excess return, then subtract realistic costs. Treat a simple retail backtest showing a Sharpe above about 2, or more than 30% a year, as evidence of a bug or overfitting. The arithmetic is sobering even at good returns:

| Capital | 5% / yr | 8% / yr | 10% / yr | After Portugal 28% tax (10% case) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| €5,000 | €250 | €400 | €500 | €360 |
| €20,000 | €1,000 | €1,600 | €2,000 | €1,440 |
| €50,000 | €2,500 | €4,000 | €5,000 | €3,600 |

Taxes favour exactly the low turnover the evidence recommends. Portugal taxes securities gains at a flat 28%. Since 2024 it excludes 10–30% of the gain on holdings of two years or more ([Rankia PT](https://www.rankia.pt/fiscalidade/fiscalidade-das-acoes-em-portugal/)), and crypto held for 365 days or more is exempt ([Portal das Finanças](https://info.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/apoio_contribuinte/Folhetos_informativos/Documents/Criptoativos.pdf)). Brazil taxes swing trades at 15%, with an exemption when monthly stock sales stay at or below R$20k, and day trades at 20% ([InfoMoney](https://www.infomoney.com.br/guias/declarar-acoes-imposto-de-renda-ir/)). A realistic success criterion for the paper agent is therefore **8–12% a year gross, a Sharpe of 0.5–1.0 after costs, and a maximum drawdown of 20% or less, all measured against SPY or an MSCI ACWI ETF with the same capital**. Those targets are inferences, not sourced norms, and even meeting them would put the agent ahead of most active managers.

## Monthly trend rules earn their keep by cutting drawdowns, not boosting returns

Trend following has the deepest evidence of any strategy in this playbook. Hurst, Ooi and Pedersen blended 1-, 3- and 12-month time-series momentum across 67 futures markets from 1880 to 2016. After simulated 2-and-20 fees and trading costs, the blend was **positive in every decade** and gained in **8 of the 10 largest 60/40 drawdowns** ([SSRN 2993026](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2993026)). The underlying signal is Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen's result: across 58 futures, an asset's own past 12-month excess return predicts its next month, and a diversified, volatility-scaled portfolio does best in extreme markets ([MOP 2012](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X11002613)). The live record is humbler. The **SG Trend Index was down about 15% over the twelve months to June 2025**, with a drawdown of about 20.6% ([Top Traders Unplugged](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/trend-following-performance-report-june-2025/)), and returns differ widely even among trend CTAs ([CFM](https://www.cfm.com/steady-trends-the-reality-of-cta-return-dispersion/)). Trend pays in sustained moves in either direction (2008, 2014, 2022). It bleeds in choppy markets and sharp V-shaped reversals, such as the post-2009 QE years and the spring-2025 tariff shock-and-rebound.

**Faber's 10-month SMA rule is the retail form of this evidence and should be the agent's core.** Five asset classes (US stocks, foreign developed stocks, 10-year Treasuries, commodities and REITs) each get 20%. At month-end, each is held if its price is above its 10-month simple moving average and moved to T-bills otherwise ([Faber SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=962461)). The 1972–2005 backtest showed a CAGR of 11.7%, a Sharpe of 0.81 and a maximum drawdown of 9.5%. Out of sample, from 2006 to March 2025, it delivered a **CAGR of 6.05%, a Sharpe of 0.68 and a maximum drawdown of 11.7%** through the global financial crisis, COVID and 2022 ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.substack.com/p/global-tactical-asset-allocation)). The return roughly halved, as McLean and Pontiff would predict, but the drawdown protection survived, and that protection is the point. CXO Advisory notes that the original evidence is gross of costs ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/long-term-outperformance-from-trends-defined-by-moving-averages/)). With five ETFs and one to three switches per asset per year, costs are negligible, but short-term gains tax is not. Fractional shares make the rule feasible even at $1,000.

**Antonacci's Global Equities Momentum (GEM)** is a complementary monthly rule. If SPY's 12-month return beats T-bills, hold whichever of SPY or VEU (ex-US stocks) has the higher 12-month return; otherwise hold AGG bonds ([BestFolio GEM](https://bestfolio.app/strategies/gem)). A vendor backtest for 1986–2026 shows a **12.3% CAGR, a −33.7% maximum drawdown and a Sharpe of 0.99** ([BestFolio](https://bestfolio.app/strategies/gem)). Treat that as optimistic, because the cost treatment is unstated and results depend on the lookback and on which day of the month it rebalances ([ReSolve](https://investresolve.com/global-equity-momentum-executive-summary/)). GEM holds one asset, so a single bad signal matters. It whipsaws in fast V-shaped selloffs, and in 2022 its bond "safe haven" fell too. **Keller and Keuning's VAA and DAA** add breadth-based crash protection. They use a fast "13612W" momentum score, 12×r1m + 4×r3m + 2×r6m + r12m, and move to defensive bonds when any offensive asset (VAA) or the canary assets VWO and BND (DAA) turn negative ([penny-vault](https://github.com/penny-vault/vigilant-asset-allocation); [DAA SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/SSRN_ID3307823_code1935527.pdf?abstractid=3212862)). Their backtest drawdowns are very low, but the rules were optimized in-sample and spend long stretches defensive. The agent should use the canary signal only as a regime input, not as a standalone sleeve.

**The original Turtle rules are fully codeable but a poor fit for a small account.** System 1 enters on a 20-day breakout and exits on a 10-day opposite breakout. System 2 uses 55-day and 20-day channels. Each unit risks 1% of equity per N, where N is a 20-day ATR, with a 2N stop and up to four units added every ½N ([TurtleTrader rules](https://www.turtletrader.com/rules/); [original rules PDF](https://oxfordstrat.com/coasdfASD32/uploads/2016/01/turtle-rules.pdf)). The edge came from dozens of diversified futures and a few huge winners, and no rigorous post-1990 cost-inclusive test of the exact rules was found ([QuantifiedStrategies](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/turtle-trading-strategy/)). One E-mini S&P contract with N of 60–100 points puts $3,000–5,000 at risk per N. That requires an account of about $300,000 to size at 1%, so a $5,000–25,000 account captures little of the diversification that made the rules work. Covel's *Trend Following* argues the case with manager track records, not controlled tests. What the agent should keep from the Turtles is the machinery: ATR-based sizing, pre-set stops, channel exits and the discipline to take every signal.

**Cross-sectional stock momentum** (buying past 12-month winners, skipping the latest month) has survived decades out of sample ([Springer review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-022-00417-8)), and trading costs erode it only at multi-billion-dollar scale ([Korajczyk & Sadka](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/korajczy/htm/wp289.pdf); [Novy-Marx & Velikov](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/ToAatTC.pdf)). Its tail risk is severe. The long–short factor **lost 45.6% in March–April 2009**. Crashes cluster in "panic states": after market declines with high volatility, beaten-down high-beta losers rebound hard. Scaling momentum exposure by forecast volatility roughly **doubled its Sharpe** ([Daniel & Moskowitz](https://www.kentdaniel.net/papers/published/jfe_16.pdf)). The long-only retail version crashes less, but it carries more market beta. It is practical as a top-10 ranking of large caps, rebalanced monthly and switched off by the regime filter. Daily cadence adds nothing to a 12-1 signal.

## Two daily-cadence edges survive: RSI(2) dips on ETFs and gated breakouts

Most published edges are monthly or annual. A daily agent is tempted to trade daily, and the evidence says that temptation is where retail money goes to die. Two rule families justify daily checks.

**Connors RSI(2) mean reversion on index ETFs is the best daily-cadence fit in this playbook.** The rule buys when the close is above the 200-day SMA and the 2-period RSI is below 10 (below 5 in aggressive versions), and exits when the close crosses above the 5-day SMA. It trades liquid ETFs such as SPY, QQQ, IWM and DIA, with signals on the close ([StockCharts ChartSchool](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/trading-strategies-and-models/trading-strategies/rsi-2)). A practitioner backtest on SPY since 1993 reports about **9% a year while invested only about 28% of the time, with a maximum drawdown of about 34%**. By that account it has stayed profitable since publication, working best in steady uptrends and worst in high-volatility trending declines ([QuantifiedStrategies](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/rsi-2-strategy-explained-larry-connors)). No peer-reviewed, cost-inclusive post-2015 test exists. The cost assumptions behind those figures are unverified, and the 34% drawdown matters: the original rule has no stop, so crashes that begin above the 200-day SMA, like February 2020, produce the worst trades. Bollinger lower-band entries, "Double 7s" and cumulative-RSI variants are the same trade under other names. The agent should run one of them, not stack all of them. The academic cousin, the cross-sectional short-term reversal anomaly, disappears after costs in small caps. It survives at only 30–50 bp a week in large caps with turnover control ([De Groot, Huij & Zhou](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378426611002263)). That makes the diversified ETF version, with spreads near 1 bp and no single-company blowups, the right implementation. The agent should add a disaster stop (for example 3 ATR) and a 10-day time stop the original lacks, accepting a small cost in backtested return for survivable tails. That modification is an inference and must be validated before use.

**Growth breakouts in the O'Neil and Minervini tradition are feasible but the least validated of the four sleeves.** CAN SLIM grew out of O'Neil's study of big winners from 1953 to 1993 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAN_SLIM)). An academic test found market-adjusted abnormal returns of about 1.81% a month on S&P 500 stocks from 1984 to 1992, a short, pre-publication-era sample ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326548848_OUTPERFORMING_THE_BROAD_MARKET_AN_APPLICATION_OF_CAN_SLIM_STRATEGY)). Minervini's Trend Template is codeable:

- price above the 150- and 200-day SMAs;
- 150-day above 200-day;
- 200-day rising for at least a month;
- 50-day above both the 150- and 200-day;
- price above the 50-day;
- at least 25–30% above the 52-week low and within 25% of the 52-week high;
- relative-strength rank of 70 or higher.

([StockGeniuses](https://stockgeniuses.com/blog/mark-minervini-trend-template/))

A large but non-peer-reviewed test of 44,475 breakouts from 1985 to 2026 found two decisive filters. The profit factor rose from 1.75 to 2.42 as relative strength rose, and **fell to 1.16 when the 200-day SMA was declining** ([nipunhsud/signal](https://github.com/nipunhsud/signal/pull/9)). Those figures are likely gross of costs. The market-direction "M" filter is the one element every source agrees on. A codeable version is:

- universe filter: the Trend Template plus quarterly EPS growth of at least 25% year on year;
- entry: a close above a 20–50-day pivot high on volume at least 1.5× the 50-day average;
- initial stop: 7–8% below entry, or below the base low;
- exit: trail with the 10-day low or the 50-day SMA;
- gate: trade only when SPY is above its 200-day SMA.

Win rates of 35–50% and dependence on a few large winners are normal, so the sleeve needs strict per-trade risk and many trades before it can be judged. **Darvas boxes** are the same idea as a Donchian breakout on a single stock and have no independent evidence.

**Pairs trading and single-stock reversal do not belong in a small account.** Gatev, Goetzmann and Rouwenhorst's distance-method pairs earned up to 11% a year over 1962–2002. Do and Faff found profits declining after 2002 as historical substitutes stopped behaving like substitutes ([Do & Faff](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228641482_Does_Naive_Pairs_Trading_Still_Work)). Both strategies also need shorting, borrow and many simultaneous positions. A long-only cash account cannot run them, and the decay argues against building the plumbing.

## Value and quality belong in slow ETF sleeves, not daily trades

Classic value screens are codeable and intellectually sound, but the recent evidence and a daily cadence both argue against making them the agent's engine. **Greenblatt's Magic Formula** ranks stocks by EBIT/enterprise value plus return on capital, excluding financials and utilities, and holds the top 20–30 for a year. Its 1988–2004 backtest showed about 30.8% a year against 12.3% for the S&P 500 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_formula_investing)). It outperformed in several European samples ([Poznań review](https://www.journals.ue.poznan.pl/REF/article/view/2790)), but practitioners report it has **lagged the market for more than a decade** ([QuantifiedStrategies](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/the-magic-formula-strategy/)). **Piotroski's F-Score** (nine binary accounting signals within the cheapest book-to-market quintile) fares worse as a standalone signal. A Compustat replication of the long–short version found it **lost about 9.5% a year over the last decade** ([Portfolio123](https://blog.portfolio123.com/why-piotroskis-f-score-no-longer-works/)). A 1963–2022 study found that the F-Score and similar formulas earn returns mainly through exposure to known style factors ([Springer RQFA 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11156-024-01331-y)). It is useful as a quality filter (F ≥ 7), not as alpha. **Graham's net-nets** (market cap at or below two-thirds of current assets minus total liabilities) posted spectacular historical results: 29.4% a year against 11.5% in Oppenheimer's test, and 35.3% a year over 1983–2008 in Carlisle et al. ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/282969845_Testing_Benjamin_Graham's_net_current_asset_value_model)). Those are equal-weighted micro-cap returns before spreads and impact. US net-nets are now scarce and often OTC-listed or foreign-domiciled, which brings fraud risk and trading access a paper agent on Alpaca cannot model credibly. Graham's defensive screen (current ratio of at least 2, ten years of positive earnings, 20 years of dividends, P/E × P/B ≤ 22.5) is a reasonable quality-value filter with no strong standalone evidence.

The factor evidence behind these screens is real but slow and cyclical. Value endured its worst drawdown on record from roughly 2007 to 2020. It then rebounded, and **MSCI World ex-USA Value beat Growth by about 21% in 2025** ([MSCI](https://www.msci.com/research-and-insights/blog-post/international-value-has-outshone-us-growth)). AQR argues that wide value spreads leave the comeback "in its early innings" ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/White-Papers/Value-Why-Now-Capturing-the-Comeback-in-Its-Early-Innings)). Profitability is the most robust addition. Novy-Marx's gross profits to assets predicts returns about as well as book-to-market and markedly improves value among large, liquid stocks ([Novy-Marx](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/AQR-Insight-Award/2012/The-Other-Side-of-Value.pdf)). Quality beat junk in 24 countries ([CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-magazine/2014/quality-control)). Betting-against-beta is weaker than it looks: transaction costs cut its profits by almost 60%, and it loses significance once other factors are controlled for ([arXiv 2409.00416](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.00416v1)). Covered-call "income" funds trailed their underlying indexes by about 2.6 points a year ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/covered-calls/)). For a small account, the right implementation is to hold the equity slots of the core sleeve through cheap value, quality or multi-factor ETFs where the owner wants a factor tilt, rebalanced annually. The agent gains nothing from checking them daily, and a stock-level screen of 20–30 names adds tracking and tax complexity for a premium that arrives, if at all, over five to ten years.

## Crypto trend works in backtests, and carry has compressed toward T-bills

Crypto is where trend following posts its most impressive numbers and where sample risk is greatest. Zarattini, Pagani and Barbon combined Donchian channels over nine lookbacks, with sizing targeted at 25% annualized volatility, on a rotating basket of the top 20 liquid coins from January 2015 to March 2025. They report a **CAGR of about 30% and a Sharpe of about 1.58 net of 0.10–0.50% fees** ([SSRN 5209907](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/5209907.pdf?abstractid=5209907&mirid=1)). A time-series momentum preprint on six crypto assets reports 18.0% a year (Sharpe 0.82) before the US spot-bitcoin ETFs launched and 28.6% (Sharpe 1.22) after. Before the ETFs, it trailed buy-and-hold by 7.7 points ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/19671502)). These samples contain only a handful of boom-bust cycles, and most studies are unrefereed. The honest reading is that long/flat trend rules in BTC and ETH mainly **avoid the 70–80% bear markets** of 2018 and 2022 while giving up part of the bull runs. After the McLean–Pontiff haircut, a Sharpe near 0.7–0.8 is a planning figure. That figure is an inference, not a measured result. The **cash-and-carry basis trade** (long spot, short perpetual, collect funding) paid about 20–25% annualized at its 2024 peaks. It compressed to about 10% or less through 2025 as arbitrage capital arrived ([bit.com](https://www.bit.com/insights/knowledge-hub/basis-trade); [BitMEX](https://www.bitmex.com/blog/2025q3-derivatives-report)). Its net return now sits near T-bills much of the time, US retail access to perpetuals is restricted, and exchange-failure risk dominates. It does not belong in this playbook. For a Portuguese resident, the 365-day crypto exemption makes a long-lookback, low-turnover trend rule markedly more tax-efficient than a fast one.

## A four-sleeve playbook with code-enforced risk and regime gates

The scorecard below condenses the evidence. "Planning return" applies the halve-the-excess rule to the best available out-of-sample or post-publication figure. These are inferences for budgeting, not forecasts.

| Strategy | Cadence | Best post-publication / cost-aware evidence | Planning return / max DD | Small-account fit | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Faber GTAA-5 (10-month SMA) | Monthly | OOS 2006–2025: 6.05% CAGR, 11.7% DD | 5–7% / 15–20% | Excellent (5 ETFs) | **Core** |
| GEM dual momentum | Monthly | Vendor 12.3%, −33.7% DD; spec-sensitive | 6–9% / 30–35% | Excellent (3 ETFs) | Core blend, ≤ half of core |
| VAA/DAA canary | Monthly | In-sample optimized | — | Excellent | Regime input only |
| RSI(2) on index ETFs | Daily | Practitioner: ~9%/yr at 28% exposure, 34% DD | 3–5% on sleeve capital / 25–35% | Excellent | **Satellite** |
| Minervini/CAN SLIM breakouts | Daily | Gross, non-peer-reviewed; M filter decisive | Unknown; high variance | Good at $5k+ | Small satellite, probation |
| Cross-sectional momentum, long-only | Monthly | Robust decades OOS; −45.6% crash (L/S) | Market ± a few % | Fair (top-10) | Optional, regime-gated |
| Crypto BTC/ETH long/flat trend | Daily | Net-of-fee Sharpe ~1.2–1.6 (short samples) | Sharpe ~0.7 / 30–40% | Good | Optional ≤10% |
| Turtle futures | Daily | No verified post-1990 test | — | Poor below ~$300k | Borrow the mechanics only |
| Magic Formula / F-Score / Graham | Annual | Lagging 10+ yrs; F-Score L/S negative | ≈ value factor | Fair | Via ETFs, not traded |
| Pairs, single-stock reversal | Daily/weekly | Decayed; needs shorts | — | Poor | **Excluded** |
| Covered calls, crypto basis | Continuous | −2.6 pts/yr; basis ≈ T-bills | — | Poor | **Excluded** |

The recommended allocation for a $5,000–25,000 paper account has four sleeves with fixed ceilings. **Sleeve A, the trend core (50–60% of capital),** is Faber GTAA-5 using liquid ETFs (for example SPY, EFA, IEF, DBC and VNQ, with SGOV or BIL as cash). The agent may optionally split this sleeve 50/50 with GEM, evaluated on the last trading day of the month. **Sleeve B, dip-buying (15–25%),** runs RSI(2) long-only on SPY, QQQ, IWM and DIA, with at most two positions open at once. **Sleeve C, momentum/breakout (10–20%),** runs Trend Template breakouts on S&P 500 or Nasdaq-100 members at 0.5% risk per trade, with at most five positions. It stays on probation until it logs 100 trades with positive expectancy. **Sleeve D, crypto trend (0–10%),** holds BTC and ETH long/flat on a Donchian ensemble with inverse-volatility sizing and is enabled only if the owner opts in. Anything not deployed sits in T-bill ETFs. Within these ceilings, code weights sleeves by inverse realized volatility, so each contributes roughly equal risk. Claude does not choose the weights freehand.

Regime detection must be deterministic and simple, because the literature's regime effects are coarse. Code computes four signals each evening:

- **trend:** SPY relative to its 200-day SMA (daily) and its 10-month SMA (month-end);
- **volatility:** SPY's 20-day realized volatility relative to its one-year median;
- **breadth:** the share of the stock universe above its own 200-day SMA, plus whether the DAA canaries VWO and BND both have positive 13612W momentum;
- **momentum-crash state:** a negative 24-month market return combined with volatility in its top quintile, following Daniel & Moskowitz's panic states.

The table below maps those signals to sleeve permissions:

| Regime (code-classified) | Sleeve A trend core | Sleeve B RSI(2) | Sleeve C breakouts | Sleeve D crypto |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bull, calm (SPY > 200d, vol ≤ median) | On (rules decide) | On | On, full risk | Per own BTC trend signal |
| Bull, volatile (SPY > 200d, vol > median) | On | On, half size | On, half risk | Per own signal, half size |
| Bear / downtrend (SPY < 200d) | On (rules move to cash/bonds) | **Off** | **Off** | Per own signal |
| Panic rebound (momentum-crash state) | On | Off | **Off** (crash risk) | Per own signal, half size |
| Choppy (≥ 3 SPY 200d crossings in 60 days) | On | On, half size | **Off** | Per own signal |

Sitting out is a rule, not a mood. The agent holds cash whenever a sleeve's gate is off. It also sits out when any of the following holds:

- a drawdown breaker trips;
- a sleeve's live results fall below the 5th percentile of its bootstrapped backtest paths;
- data is stale or two price sources disagree;
- for Sleeve C, a company reports earnings within the holding window of a new entry.

The earnings rule is a design choice, not a sourced finding.

The risk rules below operationalize Van Tharp's R-multiples, the Turtles' ATR sizing and the Market Wizards' consensus. Seykota reduced good trading to cutting losses, and Tudor Jones put defence first at about 1% risk per trade ([TurtleTrader quotes](https://www.turtletrader.com/market-quotes/); [Van Tharp Institute](https://vantharpinstitute.com/tharp-think-trading-concepts/)). The rules also reflect the volatility-management evidence: scaling exposure down when variance is high raised Sharpe ratios across market, value and momentum factors ([Moreira & Muir](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12513)). They live in the deterministic risk engine, and Claude cannot edit them.


Kelly appears only as an upper bound. Full Kelly maximizes long-run growth but produces bets far too large when the estimated edge is noisy ([MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1797366)). Half-Kelly keeps about three-quarters of the growth rate while roughly halving volatility. At 0.5% risk, fifteen consecutive losses, which is statistically ordinary for a 40%-win-rate breakout system, cost only about 7%. That margin is what keeps the agent alive long enough for its edge to show.

Validation gates are part of the risk system, because overfitting is the most common way a backtest lies. Code logs every parameter set Claude tries. Bailey and López de Prado showed that with five years of data, testing more than about **45 configurations nearly guarantees an in-sample Sharpe of 1 with zero expected out-of-sample value** ([AMS Notices](https://www.ams.org/notices/201405/rnoti-p458.pdf)). A new rule therefore needs a deflated Sharpe of at least 0.95 ([Bailey & López de Prado](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2460551)) and a t-statistic of at least 3 ([Harvey, Liu & Zhu](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/29/1/5/1843824)) on walk-forward, cost-inclusive data. It then needs 3–6 months or 50 or more paper trades before it gets capital. Only the four sleeves above, with their published parameters, start in production. That is deliberate: fixed, pre-registered rules have close to zero trials to deflate.

Claude's role follows from all this and from the architecture already chosen: Claude proposes, and deterministic code enforces. Each evening Claude reads the code-computed regime table, sleeve signals, positions and trade log. It does four things. It writes a short regime narrative that flags anything the rules cannot see: scheduled macro events, an earnings date inside a breakout's window, data anomalies, or a position whose thesis a filing has broken. It proposes which Sleeve C candidates to take when more signals fire than slots allow, ranked by relative strength and base quality. It may request a de-risking change within code-defined bounds, such as skipping an entry or halving a sleeve, but never an increase above policy. And it writes the post-trade review in R-multiples, measuring the process rather than single outcomes, as Mark Douglas and the Market Wizards advise.

Claude may not change parameters, add strategies, override stops, average down or raise risk after losses. The Market Wizards interviewees won with opposite styles but shared strict risk control and consistency ([Arvy on Market Wizards](https://arvy.ch/en/market-wizards-series-jack-schwager/)). An LLM's equivalent of tilt is a persuasive-sounding exception, and the policy file exists to refuse it.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the question from "which strategy wins" to "which failure modes does each strategy expose." Every edge in this playbook works mostly by avoiding a disaster. Trend rules sidestep the long bear markets that wreck buy-and-hold, RSI(2) harvests panics inside uptrends, and the regime gate keeps momentum away from its crash states. Returns are therefore lumpy and concentrated in rare periods, and two or three flat years are the expected cost of admission, not a sign the rules are broken. That argues for judging the agent over at least one full cycle and on drawdown-adjusted terms, and for resisting the pull to "fix" a sleeve during an ordinary losing stretch. That pull is precisely where the Brazilian, EU and US retail data show traders destroying themselves.

For a Claude-driven agent, restraint is the edge. The decay literature says any rule Claude could discover by reading books is already partly arbitraged. The overfitting literature says any rule Claude discovers by searching data is probably false. The retail literature says activity itself is the main source of loss. The most valuable output of this project is a clean, pre-registered record of whether a small set of classic rules, executed without human error and flagged by an LLM, can match a buy-and-hold ETF at lower drawdown after costs and taxes. A result of about 7% a year with a 15% worst drawdown would be a success. Any claim of much more should trigger an audit, not a bigger position.
