## 3. Notes by claim

### X1 (SEC tick size and access fee)
Timeline from SEC documents I read:
- 18 Sep 2024, Release 34-101070: half-penny tick, access-fee cap cut, round-lot change. Original compliance date: first business day of Nov 2025 (SEC press release 2024-137).
- 31 Oct 2025, press release 2025-130 (Release 34-104172): tick and cap delayed "Until the first business day of November 2026" (and the exchange-fee rule 610(d) to Feb 2026).
- 20 Mar 2026, Release 34-105058: notice of MEMX's request to let exchanges charge up to $0.0015 for half-penny stocks.
- **11 Jun 2026, Release 34-105656: delayed again to the first business day of Nov 2027** "to allow for an orderly implementation of these Rules in light of other regulatory initiatives scheduled for the balance of 2026". The order "takes no position on the MEMX Application at this time". The same day the SEC proposed to rescind the trade-through rule (Rule 611), and Chairman Atkins wrote: "I have directed the staff to prioritize a review of Rules 610(c) and 612 of Regulation NMS by the end of the year, including whether potential changes to the access fee caps and minimum pricing increments may be appropriate."
- I found nothing newer on this topic as of 29 Sep 2026 (the SEC exemptive-orders page lists 34-105656 as the newest 610(c)/612 item; a 7 Aug 2026 entry is about a different exchange matter).

Rule details (Release 34-101070): tick is $0.005 if TWAQS is $0.015 or less, otherwise $0.01, for quotes of $1.00 or more. TWAQS is measured by the primary listing exchange over regular trading hours in two evaluation periods (Jan-Mar sets May-Oct; Jul-Sep sets Nov-Apr). Access-fee cap: 30 mils to 10 mils per share for quotes of $1.00 or more, and 0.3% to 0.1% of price below $1.00.

SPY and QQQ: the SEC wrote that, of 163 stocks priced above $250 on 30 Nov 2023, "only two ... had an average quoted spread over the previous thirty trading days of $0.015 or less", and named them: "These NMS stocks were ETFs: SPY and QQQ. As of Nov. 30, 2023, the last sale price for SPY was $454.30 and its average bid-ask spread over the previous 30 trading days was $0.0105. For QQQ ... $386.70 ... $0.0116." The researcher's own one-day quotes for 28 Sep 2026 (SPY about 1.9 cents, QQQ about 2.2 cents) would not, but that is one day (a down day) and the real test uses a full quarter. ### X2 (overnight drift)
- Blog post, not a Staff Report. Authors: Boyarchenko (NY Fed), Larsen and Whelan (Copenhagen Business School). Window: 2:00 to 3:00 a.m. ET, U.S. equity futures. "roughly 3.7 percent per annum" (chart sample 1998-2020) then "close to zero since 2021". The post also notes two NightShares ETFs launched June 2022 to capture overnight returns "were closed fourteen months later".
- Sharpe 1.1 and -0.5 are in the introduction of Staff Report 917, and Table IX agrees: the 2:00-3:00 strategy (OD) has mean 3.75% a year and Sharpe 1.10 at mid-quotes, and mean -0.59% a year and Sharpe -0.54 using best bid and ask; the 1:30-3:30 version (OD+) goes 1.30 to 0.26; the "buy the dip" version goes 1.78 to 1.10. Table IX note: "The sample period is 2004.1 - 2020.12" and returns after costs "are computed from the best bid and ask prices". The Staff Report is the 2020/2022 paper; the July 2026 item is the follow-up post.

### X3 (pre-FOMC drift)
What each source really says:
- Lucca and Moench (J. Finance 70(1):329-371): 49 bp average in the 24 hours before scheduled FOMC announcements, 131 meetings, t-statistic above 4.5, September 1994 to March 2011; "about 80% of annual realized excess stock returns".
- Kurov, Wolfe, Gilbert (FRL 40, 2021): extended to Dec 2019. For meetings with press conferences the mean pre-FOMC return was 0.445% in Apr 2011-Dec 2015 and 0.092% in Jan 2016-Dec 2019 (20 meetings each); the 2016-2019 figure is not distinguishable from ordinary days (their Wilcoxon test).
- Not in the researcher's list: Ignatieva and Ohashi (Applied Economics 57(17), Apr 2025) abstract: "We find a pre-FOMC announcement drift, signifying positive excess returns on equities prior to the FOMC announcement"; sample not shown on the RePEc page and the publisher page returned 403. Knox and Vissing-Jorgensen (Fed FEDS 2026-023) mention "the 20 bps per meeting overnight drift since March 2011" (a different window). A practitioner blog (QuantSeeker) reports a SPY strategy still earning about 4% a year to Dec 2024; I treat that as weak evidence.

### X9 (Beckmeyer, DERA)
Beckmeyer sample: Feb 2021 to Sep 2023, SPX options, retail identified through Cboe's price-improvement mechanism. Gross profit uses the mid-quote at the time of the trade and net profit uses the actual price (the authors: "we set Oj to the prevailing mid-quote ... For its net profit, we use the actual transaction price"), so "transaction costs" means the spread paid to the market maker; commissions, exchange and clearing fees are excluded ("conservative estimates"). Table 2 (units of $100,000): Panel A net -2.41, gross -0.57; Panel B net -3.50, gross -1.06. The text's "roughly 60%" therefore looks like a loose sentence; the tables give 76% and 70%. (One secondary source I saw quotes "$184,000 of the $241,000", i.e. the same 76%.) DERA: Fu (Purdue and SEC), Li (SEC), Musto (Wharton), Pearson (Illinois). DERA's argument is about non-marketable limit orders on the SPXW order book: "customers are at the BBO more than half the time in recent data", costs about half those of a marketable order. Both papers are consistent with "0DTE is expensive if you cross the spread".

### X12 (independent re-derivation)

|---|---|---|
| Spread cost, 2 cents at $766 | 0.26 bp | 0.2611 bp |
| SEC fee, $20.60 per $1M sold | 0.21 bp | 0.2060 bp |

| Hit rate, C = 0.47 bp | 65% / 57% / 53% (R3 table) | 65.26% / 56.79% / 52.73% |
| Trades for +1 bp, sd 12 bp, 80% power | about 1,100 (label "t = 3") | 1,130 at the 5% bar; **2,125 at critical t = 3**; 1,296 if the expected t-statistic is 3 |

Formula check: with hit rate p and average absolute move M, a trade wins or loses about M, so expected gross = (2p - 1) x M; setting it equal to cost C gives p = 0.5 + C / (2M). Right, as long as being right or wrong does not change the size of the move. The SEC fee is charged only on the sale, so the same 0.206 bp applies to a short round trip. FINRA's fee is about 0.0025 bp per sale at $0.000195 a share (immaterial). Side note for R3 section 5 item 5: the SEC advisory says the $20.60 rate stays "until 60 calendar days after legislation is enacted that sets the amount of the Commission's fiscal year 2027 appropriation", so it does not automatically reset on 1 October.

### X13 (independent Black-Scholes)
Closed form S N(d1) - K N(d2) with r = q = 0, cross-checked by numerical integration (0.73108 both ways) and by the rule of thumb 0.3989 S sigma sqrt(T) = 0.731. Adding r = 4% and q = 1.2% moves it to $0.734. The quoted "25-minute hold" should read "25 minutes to expiry": what sets the premium is time left, not how long you hold. The 1-cent half-spread and the 15% IV are assumptions; I have no real SPY option quotes (that needs paid OPRA data). For scale, on 28 Sep 2026 the Cboe files show VIX1D close 11.27 (which would give about $0.55, 1.8%) and VIX close 16.07.

### X14 (independent re-simulation)
Model: driftless random walk, independent normal returns (fat-tailed version also run).
- R-squared and Mann-Kendall: windows of 12, 20, 60, 78 and 390 returns (13 to 391 prices; the Mann-Kendall variant with n instead of n+1 prices is also shown), 200,000 paths for windows up to 78, 40,000 (R-squared) or 4,000 (Mann-Kendall) for 390.
- ADX(14), Wilder smoothing: 9,000 paths of 260 bars, first 80 bars dropped, bars built from 5 or 30 small steps with high and low taken over the bar including its opening price. Two separate ADX codes (a loop and a pandas version) agree to within 0.2 points of share.
- What went wrong in the researcher's ADX run (`null_sim2.py`, `sim_ohlc`): the path is already cumulative, yet the previous bar's close is added again to the highs and lows, but not to the closes. Highs and lows are therefore offset from the closes. Re-running their construction with my ADX code gives 36.7% and 34.0%, matching their 37.0% and 34.3%; building the bars correctly gives 40.2% and 37.5%, so the offset lowers the share by about 3 to 4 points. (My own first attempt double-counted the previous close in every price, which made bar returns positively autocorrelated and pushed the share to about 60%; I noticed the implausible result, fixed it, and the corrected code passes a sanity check of bar-return lag-1 autocorrelation -0.0015 and standard deviation 1.003. The first draft is kept as `x14_adx_FIRST_ATTEMPT_BUGGY_do_not_use.py`.)
- Also: leaving the opening price out of the bar's high and low (as their code does) lowers the 5-step figure to 35.0%; bars drawn from closes alone give 49%. Real minute bars have ranges wider than close-to-close moves, so 37% to 40% is the relevant band.
