# Y_checker1: independent fact-check, group Y (technical analysis, AI, hype, long-term trends)

Checked on 29 Sep 2026 (container date). Research only: nothing in /home/user/IAMGOD was edited, no git, no broker or trading API, no orders. I wrote only inside `research/verification/`. I did not open any other checker's files.

**Verdict key.** CONFIRMED = the primary source says what the claim says. CONFIRMED_BUT_DIFFERENT = mostly right, but the source says something different in a way that matters (numbers given). NOT_FOUND = no primary source located. CONTRADICTED = source says the opposite. UNREACHABLE = page blocked to me (which one is named).

**How I checked.** I downloaded the papers, filings and data myself and read the text (PDF text plus rendered table pages where the text was garbled). For the four "own calculation" claims (Y10, Y11, Y13, Y14) I wrote fresh code, did not rerun the researchers' scripts, and put my number next to theirs. The Ken French files I downloaded are byte-identical (same MD5) to the researchers' copies (CRSP vintage 202608), so any difference is method, not data.

## 0. Bottom line

31 sub-claims checked (Y-numbers split where one line held several claims): **24 CONFIRMED, 7 CONFIRMED_BUT_DIFFERENT, 0 NOT_FOUND, 0 CONTRADICTED, 0 UNREACHABLE** (several pages were blocked, but every claim was reached through another copy of the primary source; the one place I relied on a researcher-supplied copy of a paper's text is the body of Y2c, see section 4).

**Claims where the numbers or the attribution changed** (details in the table and section 3):
- Y1: the "critique" that pictures are mostly rescaling is the paper's own analysis, not an outside critique. The published Journal of Finance version DOES report net-of-cost results (equal-weight net Sharpe up to 4.0 weekly, 1.5 monthly, 0.9 quarterly); the reports said costs were "not netted out". The 175% turnover is the equal-weight figure (value-weight is 181-187%).
- Y2c (Rink): at costs of 20 bp or more per trade, 9 of 41 markets (5 of 23 developed, 4 of 18 emerging) still had significant rules. Profits mostly, not entirely, vanish.
- Y3: the body of the paper says about $0.81 billion a year (range $0.61-1.02 billion), a rough extrapolation for 2001-2006; "$1 billion" is the abstract's rounding.
- Y4d (CharXiv): 47.1% vs 80.5% is a June 2024 snapshot and is stale; 2026 leaderboards list 20 models at 82-93% (self-reported).
- Y5b: Cornell (2020) is the source of the 63.3%; he does not dispute it. Guo and Liu are the ones who dispute it.
- Y11c (Novy-Marx and Velikov): costs are 34%, 42% or 53% of gross depending on design (49% for plain decile momentum), not a stated "40-45%".
- Y13: the +0.16 to +0.20 holds only for two multi-decade windows; single decades run +0.05 to +0.29. The "-0.10 since 2010" is almost all 2020 (excluding 2020 it is about -0.03).
- Y7d: 13 of 15 days is 86.7%, not 87.6% (the arXiv abstract prints 87.6%).
- Y11b: the "-15.5%" top-decile fall is the value-weighted decile; equal-weighted it is -11.6%.

Own calculations Y10, Y11a/b, Y14 reproduce the researchers' figures (Y14: my exact value for N = 28 is 1.42 against their 1.45, same curve).

### Y10: 10-month moving-average rule (exact timing)

- Market return each month = Mkt-RF + RF (simple, total US stock market). Level index = 1.0 at end of Jun 1926, compounded monthly.
- **Signal at end of month t:** level(t) is greater than the simple average of the last 10 month-end levels (t-9 through t, including t). **Position for month t+1:** the full market return that month if the signal is on, otherwise the T-bill (RF) return that month. So the signal is always one month old when used; no look-ahead.
- First signal end-Apr 1927, so the first held month is May 1927; sample May 1927 to Aug 2026 (1,192 months). No trading costs, no taxes.
- "Calendar years": the 98 full years 1928-2025 (1927 and 2026 are partial). "Lagged" means the rule's calendar-year return is strictly below buy-and-hold's.

|---|---|---|
| Rule / buy-and-hold CAGR | 9.82% / 10.33% (10.3%) | 9.82% / 10.30% (10.33% if buy-and-hold is measured from Jan 1927 instead of May 1927) |
| Volatility | 12.5% / 18.4% | 12.5% / 18.4% |

A look-ahead version (using this month's signal for this month) gives an impossible 21.2% a year, which shows why the one-month lag matters.

### Y14: expected best luck-only Sharpe, T = 2 years (about 504 daily observations)

Zero-skill annualised Sharpe estimate has standard deviation about 1/sqrt(T) = 0.707 (assumes independent, normal returns). Expected best of N tries = E[max of N standard normals] x 0.707.

| N | E[max], exact | Sharpe, exact | Sharpe, Bailey-Lopez de Prado approximation | Reports |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 1.163 | 0.822 | 0.843 | R2 table: 0.84 |
| 8 | 1.424 | **1.007** | 1.032 | R2: "about 1.0" |
| 10 | 1.539 | 1.088 | 1.113 | R7 table: 1.11 |
| 14 | 1.703 | 1.204 | 1.229 | R2 table: 1.23 |
