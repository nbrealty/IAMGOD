# Why day traders lose, and how to stop the bot making the same mistakes

*Draft for owner review, 28 September 2026. Written for the separate minute-trading (scalping and day-trading) bot, which will run on its own Alpaca **paper** account. It pairs with "Minute trading research.md" and "Minute trading backtest results.md". Those two reports did not exist yet when this was written. Every setup in them must pass the gates below. Nothing here is implemented. No orders were placed and no code was changed.*

**How to read this report**
- Rule IDs are `MT-G#` (minute-trading guardrail). Mistake IDs are `MT-#` (human mistakes) and `AI-#` (machine and AI mistakes).
- **BUILD NOW** means code it before the bot places its first paper order. **TEST FIRST** means log it as a shadow signal with no effect on orders, until it passes gate M-12 in the Agent rulebook.
- "Verified" means the number was checked in the paper, the official page or the official abstract. **UNVERIFIED** means it came from a search summary, a press story or a secondary source, and it should not be quoted as exact.
- Rule IDs such as RISK-1, EX-5, M-11, CL-3 and G-4 point to `reports/Agent rulebook.md`. "Guide rule N" points to `reports/Agent building guide.md`.

## 1. The honest headline

**Most day traders lose money. That is the finding of the biggest studies ever done.**

- **Taiwan, 1992–2006, about 450,000 day traders a year.** In a given year only about 17–20% made money after costs. **Less than 1%** were predictably profitable year after year. The top 500 earned 61.3 basis points (bps) a day before fees and 37.9 after. The worst-ranked lost 11.5 bps a day before fees and 28.9 after. (Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean, *J. Financial Markets* 18 (2014) 1–24, verified in the PDF.) [PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/The%20Cross-Section%20of%20Speculator%20Skill.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.finmar.2013.05.006)
- **Brazil, people who started day trading mini-index futures in 2013–2015 and kept going more than 300 days.** 97% lost money. Only 1.1% (17 people) earned more than the minimum wage, and only 0.5% (8 of 1,551) more than a bank teller's starting salary. (Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti, June 2020 version, verified in the PDF.) [PDF](https://ebicapital.nl/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/day-trading.pdf), [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)
- **Taiwan again.** Day traders as a group lost money after fees in every one of the 15 years studied. More than 75% quit within two years. Yet losing traders kept trading almost as often as winning ones (95.3% against 96.4%). (Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean & Zhang, Oct 2017 version, verified.) [PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/Day%20Traders/Day%20Trading%20and%20Learning%20110217.pdf)
- **United States households, 1991–96.** The households that traded most earned 11.4% a year, against 17.9% for the market. Their stock picks were about as good as everyone else's before costs. Costs made the difference. (Barber & Odean 2000, verified.) [PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers%20current%20versions/individual_investor_performance_final.pdf)

One limit: the big day-trader studies are from Taiwan and Brazil. No US day-trader study of the same size is cited here. The US evidence above is about ordinary households who traded too much.

**Why being good at arithmetic is not an edge by itself**

You said: "AI when it comes to numbers is unmatched". It helps to split that into two things.
- **Code** (plain Python) does exact arithmetic. That is useful, but every trading firm already has it. It is not an advantage over anyone.
- **A language model** such as Claude is *not* a reliable calculator. In one 2023 test, GPT-4-Turbo with a retrieval system answered 81% of finance questions wrongly or refused to answer (FinanceBench, [arXiv 2311.11944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11944)). Today's models are better, but the failure type remains.
- **Speed.** Professional systems react to scheduled economic news within about 5 milliseconds (Chordia, Green & Kottimukkalur 2018, abstract via search summary, [link](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/31/12/4650/4956248)). A language-model call is far slower. Its exact speed was not checked here, so the bot must measure it (rule MT-G29).
- **Who you trade against.** In minute trading the other side is usually a professional with faster data, lower costs and better fills. Doing the sums right does not change that.

**What a bot CAN do better than a human**

Most of the mistakes below are failures of discipline, not of arithmetic. That is where a bot can genuinely help:
- **Discipline.** It follows the exit plan every time. It never widens a stop, never "wins it back", and never sizes up after a lucky morning.
- **No emotions.** No fear, greed, boredom or revenge.
- **Perfect records.** Every quote, order, fill, cost and reason is logged.
- **Honest measurement.** It can compare itself with random entries and with simply holding SPY, and switch itself off when it is losing.

But a bot adds new ways to fail. It can repeat one mistake thousands of times in minutes (Knight Capital lost more than $460 million in about 45 minutes). It can fool itself with a backtest that tried too many versions. A language model can "remember" the past it is being tested on. Section 3 covers these.

**Bottom line.** The rules in section 4 cannot create an edge. They make losing slower, smaller and easier to see. The most likely honest outcome is that no minute setup passes the gates. If so, the right move is to trade nothing, and that counts as the system working.

## 2. Human mistakes

| ID | Mistake | What it looks like | Evidence (with numbers) | How it loses money |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MT-1 | **Overtrading: costs eat the profits** | Many trades a day. Constant switching between stocks. | US households 1991–96 (66,465 households). Those trading most (turnover above 8.8% a month, over 250% a year) earned 11.4% a year net, against 17.9% for the market and 18.5% for those trading least. Gross returns were similar. Average household: 16.4% net. [Barber & Odean 2000](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers%20current%20versions/individual_investor_performance_final.pdf) (verified). Stocks bought did 3.3% worse (raw) and 3.2% worse (market-adjusted) over the next year than stocks sold, before costs. [Odean 1999](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers%20current%20versions/doinvestors.pdf) (verified). | Every round trip pays the spread plus fees. On a minute chart the expected move is tiny, so 1–2 cents a share of cost can be most of the edge. More trades with no more skill just multiply the cost. |
| MT-2 | **Ignoring the base rate** | "Other people lose, but my method is different." | Taiwan: 17–20% profitable in a given year, under 1% predictably profitable; top 500 +37.9 bps a day after fees, bottom −28.9 bps ([Barber et al. 2014](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/The%20Cross-Section%20of%20Speculator%20Skill.pdf), verified). Brazil (June 2020 version): of those who persisted more than 300 days, 97% lost money, 1.1% beat the minimum wage, 0.5% beat a bank teller's salary ([PDF](https://ebicapital.nl/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/day-trading.pdf), verified). The 2019 RePEc version says 0.4%, and the best trader earned US$310 a day with a standard deviation of US$2,560 ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/spa/wpaper/2019wpecon47.html)). | Money and effort go into a game whose average player loses after costs. A bot with no edge loses steadily, and faster at minute frequency. |
| MT-3 | **Not learning: carrying on after proven losses** | Blame bad luck, tweak the method, try again. | Taiwan 1992–2006: group lost money after fees in each of 15 years; more than 75% quit within two years; unprofitable traders with 50+ days of experience still had a 95.3% chance of trading again in the next 12 months, against 96.4% for profitable ones ([Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean & Zhang 2017](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/Day%20Traders/Day%20Trading%20and%20Learning%20110217.pdf), verified). Brazil: "no evidence of learning by day trading" ([RePEc 2019](https://ideas.repec.org/p/spa/wpaper/2019wpecon47.html)). | A losing method that is never switched off keeps losing. Each tweak resets the trader's hope, not the results. |
| MT-4 | **Disposition effect: selling winners early, holding losers** | Take a small profit fast for the good feeling; hold a loser "until it comes back". | 10,000 US accounts, 1987–93: investors sold 14.8% of winning positions but only 9.8% of losing ones. Winners sold beat losers kept by 3.4 percentage points over the next year ([Odean 1998](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers%20current%20versions/areinvestorsreluctant.pdf), verified). Named by [Shefrin & Statman 1985](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1985.tb05002.x). Caveat: part of the measured effect comes mechanically from limit orders ([Linnainmaa 2010](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v65y2010i4p1473-1506.html)). | Wins are cut small and losses grow, so the average loss becomes bigger than the average win. A high win rate can still lose money. |
| MT-5 | **Revenge trading: more risk to get back to even** | Bigger bets in the afternoon after a bad morning. | Chicago Board of Trade floor traders "regularly [assume] above-average afternoon risk to recover from morning losses", and prices they set reverse faster ([Coval & Shumway 2005](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v60y2005i1p1-34.html), abstract verified). 1998 T-bond futures: 31.2% of traders with morning losses took above-average afternoon risk, against 27% of those with gains. 31.2 / 27 = 1.156, the widely quoted "about 16% more likely" ([CFA digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2005/11/do-behavioral-biases-affect-prices-digest-summary), verified). The "break-even effect" in real-money experiments: [Thaler & Johnson 1990](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/gambling-house-money-and-trying-break-even-effects-prior-outcomes-risky-choice). | Bigger, worse bets under pressure turn a small losing day into a large one. The "recovery" trades reverse, so they lose on average. |
| MT-6 | **House money and the hot hand: sizing up after wins** | "I'm on a roll, double the size." | Taiwan index options: traders "take above-average risks in afternoon trading after morning gains" ([Liu, Tsai, Wang & Zhu 2010](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v56y2010i4p606-620.html), abstract verified). Market-wide turnover rises for months after high returns ([Statman, Thorley & Vorkink 2006](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/19/4/1531/1580508), abstract via search summary). House-money effect: [Thaler & Johnson 1990](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/mnsc.36.6.643). | The biggest positions arrive just after a lucky streak, before results fall back to normal. One loss at double size wipes out several wins. |
| MT-7 | **Chasing attention: the news or the "top gainers" list** | Buying whatever is on the most-active list or in the headlines. | Individuals are net buyers of attention-grabbing stocks (in the news, abnormal volume, extreme one-day moves) ([Barber & Odean 2008](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers%20current%20versions/allthatglitters_rfs_2008.pdf), verified). Strategies following extreme retail buying earn −14.8% a year in stocks with heavy retail trading ([Barber, Lin & Odean 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v59y2024i6p2547-2581_3.html), abstract verified). | By the time it is on the list, the move has happened and the buyer pays a crowded price. The attention premium then reverses. |
| MT-8 | **Herding with apps and social media** | Piling into the stock everyone on the app or on Reddit is buying. | "Intense buying by Robinhood users forecasts negative returns. Average 20-day abnormal returns are −4.7% for the top stocks purchased each day" ([Barber, Huang, Odean & Schwarz 2022](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:77:y:2022:i:6:p:3141-3190), abstract verified). In early 2021, meme stocks rose "as bullish sentiments of individual investors filled social media", and "several retail broker-dealers temporarily prohibited certain activity" ([SEC press release 2021-212](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2021-212)). No GameStop price figures are given because none were verified. | Buying late into crowded names that then fall. Getting stuck when the broker restricts trading or the stock is halted. |
| MT-9 | **Lottery-seeking: penny stocks and stocks that just jumped** | Cheap, volatile stocks with a recent huge one-day jump. | Stocks with the biggest recent one-day jump (MAX) do worse: the gap between lowest and highest MAX groups "exceed[s] 1% per month" ([Bali, Cakici & Whitelaw 2011](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v99y2011i2p427-446.html), abstract verified). Individuals prefer lottery-type stocks, which underperform ([Kumar 2009](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v64y2009i4p1889-1933.html), abstract verified). Kumar's "2 to 3 percent less a year" comes from a [university press quote](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2009/02/090226110655.htm): **UNVERIFIED** in the paper. | Overpaying for long-shot payoffs, plus wide spreads and gap risk. |
| MT-10 | **Gambling on short-dated options** | Same-day (0DTE) or weekly calls and puts. | More than 75% of retail S&P 500 option trades are 0DTE. Retail lost about $241,000 a day on **0DTE S&P 500 options** from Feb 2021 to Sep 2023, and about $350,000 a day after daily expiries began on 16 May 2022. The paper blames single-leg, premium-paying, high-volatility trades. It says retail actually got *lower* effective spreads through price improvement ([Beckmeyer, Branger & Gayda, Dec 2023](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf), verified). On spreads: retail "prefer cheaper, weekly options with average bid-ask spread of 12.6%, and lose money on average" ([Bryzgalova, Pavlova & Sikorskaya 2023](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827/), verified). Counterpoint: a more sophisticated sample shows only "modest losses compared to wide bid-ask spreads" ([Bogousslavsky & Muravyev 2025](https://www.brettonwoodsskiconference.com/uploads/b/f9bfc8b0-0251-11ed-a646-3dea17112d2f/An%20Anatomy%20of%20Retail%20Option%20Trading.pdf), verified). Losses of 5–9% around earnings ([de Silva, Smith & So](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4050165)) and effective spreads under 40% of quoted for patient traders ([Muravyev & Pearson 2020](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v33y2020i11p4973-5014..html)) are **UNVERIFIED** (search summaries). | A 10%+ round-trip spread plus time decay means the option must move a lot just to break even. Most expire worthless or are sold at the bid. |
| MT-11 | **Leverage and margin** | Borrowing to trade bigger than the account. | The 2010 US retail forex leverage cap "reduces trading volume by 23%, alleviates high-leverage traders' losses by 40%" ([Heimer & Simsek 2019](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v132y2019i3p1-21.html), abstract verified). EU regulators found "74–89% of retail accounts typically lose money" on CFDs, with average losses of EUR 1,600 to 29,000 per client ([ESMA 2018](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/library/esma71-98-128_press_release_product_intervention.pdf), verified). FINRA Notice 26-10: the $25,000 pattern-day-trader minimum is replaced by an intraday margin standard, **effective 4 June 2026, with brokers allowed to phase it in until 20 Oct 2027**. Alpaca may still apply the old rule; check its current treatment ([FINRA 26-10](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-10), verified). | Leverage multiplies both costs and losses. Margin calls force selling at the worst prices. One gap can wipe out the account. |
| MT-12 | **Buying at the open** | Jumping in during the first minutes when prices are wild. | Overnight gains reverse during the day, driven by high opening prices in retail-attention stocks. "The additional implicit transaction costs for retail traders who buy high-attention stocks near the open frequently exceed the effective half spread" ([Berkman, Koch, Tuttle & Zhang 2012](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v47y2012i04p715-741_00.html), abstract verified). In the roughly 10 years studied, the US equity premium came overnight ([Cooper, Cliff & Gulen 2008 working paper](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1004081)); its QQQ figures (+23.7% overnight, −23.3% intraday, 1999–2006) are **UNVERIFIED** (paper not read). | Early entries pay inflated prices and the widest spreads of the day. A bot that is flat every night gives up the overnight return, so it must beat a benchmark that earns it. |
| MT-13 | **Paying the spread; stale limit orders getting picked off** | Hitting "buy now" every time; or leaving limit orders sitting for hours. | Taiwan: individuals' aggregate portfolio lost 3.8 percentage points a year (2.2% of Taiwan's GDP). The losses "can be traced to their aggressive orders"; passive orders were profitable at short horizons ([Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean 2009](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers%20current%20versions/justhowmuchdoindividualinvestorslose_rfs_2009.pdf), verified). Taiwan had no market orders, so those aggressive orders were **marketable limit orders**: they still crossed the spread. Resting limit orders "suffer from adverse selection" ([Linnainmaa 2010](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v65y2010i4p1473-1506.html), abstract verified; Finnish, daily data). | Crossing the spread on every trade is a steady cost. Resting limits fill mainly when a faster trader knows the price is about to go against you. |
| MT-14 | **Paying gurus and trusting claimed returns** | Courses, Discord groups, P&L screenshots. | Warrior Trading and its CEO agreed to pay $3 million. Ads promised stocks "that move up 20 to 30 percent" each day; according to the complaint, "the vast majority of customer accounts actually lost money" ([FTC, 19 April 2022](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/04/federal-trade-commission-cracks-down-warrior-trading-misleading-consumers-false-investment-promises)); refunds of more than $2.9 million ([FTC, Jan 2023](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/01/ftc-returns-more-29-million-consumers-harmed-warrior-trading)). Day trading for a living is "virtually impossible... contrary to what course providers claim" ([Chague et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)). | Course fees first, then losses from a method never shown to work after costs. |

## 3. Machine and AI mistakes

These are the ways a bot, a backtest or a language model fails. Some are the human mistakes above in a new form. Others only machines can make.

| ID | Mistake | What it looks like | Evidence (with numbers) | How it loses money |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AI-1 | **Runaway code (Knight Capital)** | A deploy error leaves old code live; it sends orders in a loop. | 1 Aug 2012: for "212 incoming parent orders", Knight's system "sent millions of child orders, resulting in 4 million executions in 154 stocks for more than 397 million shares in approximately 45 minutes". Net long about $3.5 billion and net short about $3.15 billion; "realized a $460 million loss". New code reached 7 of 8 servers with no second review. Before the open the system sent "97 of these e-mail messages" warning of the error. Removing the new code from the 7 good servers "worsened the problem" ([SEC order 34-70694](https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/admin/2013/34-70694.pdf), verified). $12 million penalty; controls were "not capable of preventing the entry of orders that exceeded pre-set capital thresholds" ([SEC 2013-222](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2013-222)). | One bug can lose more than the strategy could ever earn, in minutes, before a human notices. |
| AI-2 | **Orders with no price limit (Flash Crash 2010, 24 Aug 2015)** | Market or stop-market orders into a thin market. | 6 May 2010: a program sold "75,000 E-Mini contracts (valued at approximately $4.1 billion)" at 9% of the previous minute's volume "without regard to price or time"; about 35,000 contracts (about $1.9 billion) sold between 2:32 and 2:45. "Over 20,000 trades across more than 300 securities were executed at prices more than 60% away" from moments earlier, some at "a penny or less, or as high as $100,000" ([SEC/CFTC report](https://www.sec.gov/news/studies/2010/marketevents-report.pdf), verified). 24 Aug 2015: "1,278 LULD trading halts"; in NYSE Arca reopenings with sell imbalances, "94% of the total imbalances resulted from market orders"; 6 of 41 very large companies fell more than 20% soon after the open ([SEC staff note](https://www.sec.gov/marketstructure/research/equity_market_volatility.pdf), verified). | A stop meant to cap a 1% loss fills 10–60% lower, or the bot buys a spike at an absurd price. One fill can erase weeks of small gains. |
| AI-3 | **Overfitting: try many versions, keep the best** | A computer tests thousands of variants; the winner looks brilliant by chance. Accepting t ≈ 2 as "significant". | "For a model based on 5 years of data, one can be misled by looking at even as few as 45 sample configurations" ([AMS press release](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/04/140410103005.htm) on [Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu 2014](https://www.ams.org/notices/201405/rnoti-p458.pdf), *Notices of the AMS* 61(5):458; PDF returned 403, figure checked in the press release only). Hold-out tests "tend to be unreliable"; PBO/CSCV proposed ([Bailey et al. preprint](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf)). A new factor "needs to clear a much higher hurdle, with a t-ratio greater than 3.0"; "most claimed research findings in financial economics are likely false" ([Harvey, Liu & Zhu 2016](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592), abstract verified). | The live strategy is fitted to past noise. It loses on new data, often right after money is committed. Minute data gives so many bars that an overfit backtest looks statistically strong. |
| AI-4 | **Minute-chart rules with no data-snooping test** | Breakouts, VWAP bounces and indicator crosses taken at face value. | "None of the 7846 popular technical trading rules we test are profitable after data snooping bias is taken into account" ([Marshall, Cahan & Cahan 2008](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:empfin:v:15:y:2008:i:2:p:199-210), abstract verified). The detail that this used 5-minute bars on an S&P 500 ETF in 2002–03 is **UNVERIFIED**. | Many trades a day on zero edge: a steady loss equal to spread plus fees. |
| AI-5 | **Look-ahead bias in backtest code** | The backtest trades at a bar's close using that bar's high, or uses the day's final high at 10:00. AI-written code does this easily. | A deliberately cheating oracle with "a Sharpe ratio of 35" "survives Deflated Sharpe and probability-of-backtest-overfitting testing completely"; leakage-safe evaluation rejected "every LLM-discovered strategy" and "certifies passive benchmarks" ([Gencay, arXiv 2608.27734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27734), abstract read; single-author preprint, not peer reviewed). | Backtest profits that cannot be earned. The overfitting tests in AI-3 do not catch it. |
| AI-6 | **Survivorship and scanner bias** | Testing only stocks that exist today, or today's gainers list. | Correct delisting returns "are not available for most of the stocks that have been delisted for negative reasons" ([Shumway 1997](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1997.tb03818.x)). On Nasdaq a −55% correction is suggested for missing delisting returns ([Shumway & Warther 1999](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/0022-1082.00192), *J. Finance* 54(6):2361–2379, abstract verified). Rulebook C-1 already flags this for sleeve C. | The worst outcomes (−50% or more, halts, delistings) are missing, so momentum and small-cap scalps look better than they are. |
| AI-7 | **Ignoring costs, and believing paper fills and thin free data** | Paper P&L looks great; live would not. | Anomalies with high turnover rarely survive costs: "few of the strategies with higher turnover do" ([Novy-Marx & Velikov 2016](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20721)). Alpaca paper fills do not account for market impact, "price slippage due to latency", "order queue position (for non-marketable limit orders)", price improvement or regulatory fees. Fills are matched to the NBBO, can be much larger than the liquidity really available, and are partial "for a random size 10% of the time" ([Alpaca paper trading](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/paper-trading), verified; wording partly paraphrased). The free feed is IEX only: AAPL on 29 Sept 2023 had 12,630 IEX trades against 535,136+ on the consolidated SIP feed, about 2.4%. Only the most recent 15 minutes of SIP data is restricted on the free plan ([Alpaca market data FAQ](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/market-data-faq), verified). IEX quotes are not the national best bid and offer (NBBO). | A 3–5 bps gross edge becomes a loss after a 2–10 bps round-trip cost. Paper hides this, especially for the queue-dependent limit fills that scalping relies on. |
| AI-8 | **Trading a published edge as if it has not decayed** | Setups from books, YouTube or our own reports treated as fresh. | For 97 predictors, returns were "26% lower out-of-sample and 58% lower post-publication" ([McLean & Pontiff 2016](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12365)). These were monthly stock anomalies, not minute setups, so any haircut for intraday setups is an assumption. | The bot expects the historical edge and gets less than half. At minute frequency half an edge is often less than costs. |
| AI-9 | **Crowding and regime change** | Many funds with the same trade exit at once. | Quant funds had "unprecedented losses" in the week of 6 Aug 2007; at 8:1 leverage a book-to-market portfolio had "a cumulative loss of 24% by the close of August 7th"; "sharp reversals... erased nearly all of the losses" by 10 Aug, but a fund that had cut leverage "would not have been able to recoup all of its losses" ([Khandani & Lo](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w14465/w14465.pdf), verified). The Bank of England warns AI trading "could lead to firms taking increasingly correlated positions... thereby amplifying shocks" ([BoE, April 2025](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/financial-stability-in-focus/2025/april-2025); a warning, not a measured effect). | Forced out at the bottom, missing the rebound. Losses bigger than anything in the backtest. |
| AI-10 | **Trusting stale or corrupt data, position state or news text** | Acting on an old price, a double-counted position or a planted headline. | 24 Aug 2015: until about 9:42 the published S&P 500 index still used 21 Aug closing prices for NYSE stocks not yet open, so its low was only about 5.2% down while SPY hit −7.8% (E-mini −7%, SPY net asset value −8%) ([SEC staff note](https://www.sec.gov/marketstructure/research/equity_market_volatility.pdf), verified). "Small perturbations at a single component can propagate through the agent decision loop and induce extreme concentration, runaway exposure, and large portfolio drawdowns" ([TradeTrap, arXiv 2512.02261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02261), abstract). Of 15 academic LLM trading schemes, "80% fail at least one core robustness metric and 100% exhibit security vulnerabilities" ([Wang & Saxena, arXiv 2609.19705](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19705), abstract). | Buying at a price that no longer exists, doubling a position already held, or trading on a fake headline (prompt injection). |
| AI-11 | **LLM "time travel": the model already knows the past** | An LLM backtest on years it was trained on. ## 4. The guardrails

Each rule says what it prevents, how it is enforced, how to test it, its status, and how it relates to the existing rulebook. "Hard lock" means code refuses the action. Neither Claude nor a setup can override it.

**Key terms used in the rules**
- **E0**: start-of-day equity. It is taken from Alpaca's `last_equity` field (equity at the previous close), never from current equity (MT-G40).
- **R**: a trade's actual dollar risk at entry = quantity × (entry price − worst stop fill price). 1R is the planned maximum.
- **honest_pnl**: paper P&L marked down for costs and kind paper fills (MT-G4). Every gate uses it.
- **ENTRY / EXIT orders**: entry gates never block exits, stops, flattens or the kill switch (MT-G38).
- **Shadow**: the signal is logged and scored as if traded, but no order is sent.

## 5. What no rule can fix

1. **No rule creates an edge.** These rules make losing slower, smaller and easier to see. Fewer than 1% of Taiwan day traders were predictably profitable ([Barber et al. 2014](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/The%20Cross-Section%20of%20Speculator%20Skill.pdf)), and 97% of Brazilian persisters lost money ([Chague et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)). The likeliest honest result is that no minute setup passes MT-G5 to MT-G7, and then the right move is to trade nothing.
2. **Being good at numbers is not an edge.** Code does exact arithmetic; so does every trading firm. An LLM is not a reliable calculator ([FinanceBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11944)) and is far slower than markets react to news ([Chordia et al.](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/31/12/4650/4956248), abstract only). The AI's value here is discipline, not prediction.
3. **The gates may reject a real edge.** t ≥ 3, deflated Sharpe, PBO, SPA, the random baseline and the sample minimum together are strict. MT-G42 measures how strict. That is acceptable, because the default answer is "no trade", but the owner should expect it and not loosen the gates to "get a trade".
4. **Statistics cannot rule out luck.** At 100 trades a zero-edge setup can still pass and a real one can still be killed. The drift monitor trades false alarms against slow detection (section 4b). Every threshold (0.25% R, 4 trades a day, −1% day stop, 2 × cost) is a conservative design choice, not a proven optimum.
5. **Paper cannot prove live results.** Queue position, market impact and latency are not simulated ([Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/paper-trading)). MT-G4 only estimates the gap.
6. **Tail events can beat any collar.** A gap, a halt or a flash crash ([SEC/CFTC](https://www.sec.gov/news/studies/2010/marketevents-report.pdf)) can skip past a stop-limit, and the watchdog then sells into a falling market. A broker or data outage while holding a position is outside our control.
7. **Guard code can have bugs.** The locks are software too. A bug in the risk module or reconciler is exactly the Knight failure. Tests, dry runs and the outside watchdog reduce this but do not remove it.
8. **Regimes change.** Edges decay after publication ([McLean & Pontiff](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12365)) and crowded trades unwind ([Khandani & Lo](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w14465/w14465.pdf)). The drift monitor only reacts after losses.
9. **Humans can override everything.** The owner, or a later AI session, can edit caps, reset halts or skip gates. MT-G41 makes that visible; it cannot stop it.
10. **The AI evidence is thin.** The LLM bias studies are lab experiments with mixed results. The Alpha Arena figures are UNVERIFIED against nof1's own page, and sources disagree by more than 10 points on Claude's loss. Model updates can change behaviour without warning, which is why MT-G10 and MT-G32 treat a model change as a new system.
11. **The evidence is mostly not about US day traders.** The big day-trader studies are from Taiwan (1992–2006) and Brazil (2013–2015). The US studies are about households who traded too much, not day traders.
12. **Still unverified** (do not quote as exact, and the build session must check before coding):
    - Kumar's "2–3% a year"; the Cooper, Cliff & Gulen QQQ split; the data details of Marshall et al.; the de Silva et al. and Muravyev & Pearson figures; the AMS "45 configurations" (press release only); Profit Mirage's decay size; Statman et al., Chordia et al. and Busse & Green (abstracts via search summary).
    - Alpaca: whether client_order_id uniqueness is enforced; whether bracket legs reserve shares; how legs behave when an entry is partly filled and the rest cancelled; whether halt and LULD status streams exist on the free plan; API rate limits.
    - FINRA TAF and CAT fee rates; release times for 08:30 and 10:00 data and the FOMC press conference.
    - How long an LLM call takes (measure it, MT-G29).
