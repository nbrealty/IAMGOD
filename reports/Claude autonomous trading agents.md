# Let Claude propose trades, let code execute

You can build an autonomous Claude-driven trading agent in Python today, and paper trading is the right place to start. The evidence says to treat it as a research experiment, not a money machine. In every rigorous, contamination-controlled evaluation published through September 2026, most frontier LLM agents fail to beat plain buy-and-hold after costs. Claude Sonnet 4.5 lost roughly 35–42% of real capital in both public Alpha Arena seasons. Where LLM agents show any edge, it is usually lower drawdown rather than higher return. The engineering lessons, however, are consistent and actionable. Agent architecture matters more than which LLM you pick. Risk limits, execution and account state must live in deterministic code the model cannot override. Paper forward-tests are the only clean evaluation, because backtests on pre-training-cutoff data are contaminated by memorization. The recommended build is a lean, once-daily, portfolio-level loop. Haiku 4.5 summarizes each ticker, one Claude Opus 5.5 call outputs schema-validated target weights, and plain Python enforces limits and sends orders to an Alpaca paper account. Estimated LLM cost is about **$10–17 per month** for 20–50 tickers. Running this on your own account is legal without a license in the US, the EU/Portugal and Brazil. That changes the moment you trade other people's money or sell the signals.

## Frontier LLMs mostly lose to buy-and-hold in honest tests

The best-known live contest, nof1's Alpha Arena, gave each model real money and identical prompts. In **Season 1** (Oct 18 – Nov 3, 2025), six models each traded $10,000 of crypto perpetuals on Hyperliquid, and four of the six lost money. Qwen 3 Max won with **+22.31%** and DeepSeek V3.1 gained +4.89%. **Claude Sonnet 4.5 finished third with about $5,799, a loss of roughly 42%**. Gemini 2.5 Pro, Grok 4 and GPT-5 did worse ([ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/); [GNcrypto](https://www.gncrypto.news/news/qwen-wins-alpha-arena-season-1-with-22-percent-returns/)). **Season 1.5** moved to US equities and ended December 3, 2025. It added a "Monk Mode" that capped trade frequency and position size. A then-unreleased Grok 4.20 led, reported at +22.38% (other coverage gives 12.11%, probably a different aggregation). **Claude Sonnet 4.5 finished seventh of eight at −35.08%** ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/alpha-arena-1-5-season-results-grok-4-20-leads-with-22-38-return); [GNcrypto](https://www.gncrypto.news/news/mystery-model-alpha-arena-season-1-5-winner/)). The Season 1 crypto champion Qwen crashed on equities ([traderank.ai](https://www.traderank.ai/blog/alpha-arena-alternatives-2026)), and as of August 2026 no Season 2 had been published. The ranking reversals are the real lesson. Each season is one two-week sample, and the winners changed with the market regime. Alpha Arena shows how fragile these agents are, not which model trades best.

Academic benchmarks that control for data contamination reach the same verdict more carefully. **StockBench** (October 2025) tested GPT-5, Claude-4, o3, Qwen3, Kimi-K2, GLM-4.5 and DeepSeek. It used 20 Dow Jones stocks over 82 trading days after the models' training cutoffs and found that "most LLM agents struggle to outperform the simple buy-and-hold baseline" ([arXiv 2510.02209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02209)). The best agent, Qwen3-235B-Instruct, made **+2.4% against +0.4%** for equal-weight buy-and-hold, with a shallower drawdown (−11.2% vs −15.2%). Reasoning ("Think") variants did no better than instruct variants ([Neurohive](https://neurohive.io/en/news/kimi-k2-and-qwen3-235b-ins-best-ai-models-for-stock-trading-chinese-researchers-found/)). **FINSABER** (a KDD 2026 oral paper) is the most damaging study. It covers 20 years, more than 100 symbols including delisted names, and models slippage and LLM costs. Previously reported LLM advantages "deteriorate significantly" under that test. The LLM strategies were too timid in bull markets and too aggressive in bear markets ([arXiv 2505.07078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07078)). In a secondary summary of its results, buy-and-hold had the best composite Sharpe (**0.703**) and neither FinMem nor FinAgent produced statistically significant alpha (**p > 0.34**) ([Lacuna summary](https://lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/work/can-llm-based-financial-investing-strategies-outperform-the-market-in-long-run/wrk_7fd368a3bf0f28ca6008b4c9bdf5a866)).

The optimistic results come from framework papers with narrow tests. **TradingAgents** reports superior Sharpe and roughly 23–27% cumulative returns. The test covered only AAPL, GOOGL and AMZN from June to November 2024 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20138); [beginnersinai](https://beginnersinai.org/tradingagents-explained/)), a window that overlaps the training data of its GPT-4o-era backbones. The live **Agent Market Arena** benchmark (ACM WebConf 2026) is more encouraging. It tested four agent designs across GPT-4o, GPT-4.1, Claude-3.5-haiku, Claude-sonnet-4 and Gemini-2.0-flash on BTC, ETH, TSLA and BMRN, and found that agents "often" beat buy-and-hold. Its most useful finding is that **agent architecture moved results more than the choice of LLM**, and no single model or agent won on every asset ([arXiv 2510.11695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11695)). **LiveTradeBench** ran 21 LLMs live for 50 days and found that general-reasoning leaderboard scores do not predict trading results ([arXiv 2511.03628](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03628)). A 2026 hybrid of LLM specialists and rule-based signals won the FinMMEval live TSLA task with +13.51% against −14.7% for buy-and-hold ([arXiv 2607.12233](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.12233)). That result is a single short window. No audited long-run live track record of an LLM-run strategy exists, and no rigorous P&L evaluation of Claude Opus 4.x/5.x was found. The honest prior for your project is that the agent will roughly track its market exposure minus costs. Beating that is the hypothesis you are testing.

## Seven failure modes explain most of the losses

The documented failures are specific, and each one has an engineering countermeasure.

**Leverage and weak risk control** caused the Alpha Arena wipeouts. Even the leaders ran 12–17x leverage, and Claude, Gemini and GPT-5 suffered major drawdowns attributed to over-leveraging ([iWeaver](https://www.iweaver.ai/blog/alpha-arena-ai-trading-season-1-results/); [iWeaver leaderboard](https://www.iweaver.ai/blog/alpha-arena-ai-trader-showdown/)).

**Overtrading** is the next biggest drain. Gemini 2.5 Pro traded most and paid **$1,284 in fees on a $10,000 account** in about two weeks. The winner, Qwen, made only about 43 trades ([Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/alpha-arena-nof1-ai-explained)). Practitioners concluded that the winners won through discipline: fewer trades, smaller size and faster loss-cutting, not better prediction ([PickMyTrade](https://blog.pickmytrade.io/claude-vs-chatgpt-vs-gemini-vs-grok-trading-2026/)). Nof1's own Monk Mode, which capped frequency and size, reflects the same lesson.

**Directional bias** is a Claude-specific finding. nof1 observed that Claude Sonnet 4.5 "rarely ever shorts" ([nof1 TechPost1](https://nof1.ai/blog/TechPost1)). In a long-only paper account this matters less, but the agent must be told explicitly that holding cash is a valid defensive choice.

**Regime misbehavior** means being too cautious in bull markets and too aggressive in bear markets ([FINSABER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07078)).

**Run-to-run inconsistency** is structural. Agents given identical conditions reach different decisions, with large variation in returns ([TrustTrade, arXiv 2603.22567](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.22567)), and financial reasoning is sensitive to prompt wording ([ATLAS, arXiv 2510.15949](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15949v1)).

**Look-ahead bias and memorization** make LLM backtests unreliable. A model trained through 2024 already "knows" what happened after a 2022 prompt date ([HedgeFundAlpha](https://hedgefundalpha.com/education/your-llms-alpha-might-be-mere-memorization/)). The effect is stronger for larger models and for indexes, and models recall pre-cutoff values verbatim ([arXiv 2512.23847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23847)). Glasserman and Lin also found a "distraction effect" from general company knowledge ([arXiv 2309.17322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17322)).

**Corrupted state** is the most dangerous failure. The TradeTrap stress test injected fake news, hijacked MCP tools to return false prices, and tampered with memory and position records. A tiny account-state error drove about **61% loss with 100% exposure to one stock**, because agents "blindly trust" their state ([arXiv 2512.02261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02261)).

Builders' postmortems add the operational layer. One practitioner lost 40% live after backtests showed 65–80% win rates, and live trading delivered 23%. His conclusion was blunt: "LLMs are for analysis and code is for execution … hard-code your risk management" ([Medium, J. Dolejs](https://medium.com/@kojott/i-lost-40-of-my-trading-account-by-trusting-an-ai-and-how-i-fixed-it-91b63d2f565a)). He later adopted a **90-day paper-trading rule** after a zero-quantity position bug locked up capital ([Medium](https://medium.com/@kojott/why-llm-trading-backtests-need-two-weeks-and-twenty-dollars-5a19a525a095)). Another bot reported four days of trading that never happened, which was a monitoring gap, not a strategy flaw ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/jugeni/60-of-my-921-wasnt-strategy-the-other-40-wasnt-even-visible-4m30)). A rigorous negative result is instructive. One pipeline used walk-forward validation, multiple-testing correction and cost sensitivity. Its gross edge of about 5.5 bps/day sat below a 6–10 bps breakeven, so it ran at **−10.1%/yr net against +18.1%/yr buy-and-hold**, and zero of 49 features survived correction ([GitHub postmortem](https://github.com/AbhayPhalswal/llm-stock-predictor-postmortem)). One builder whose bots beat the benchmark declared the win invalid under success criteria written in advance ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/nunc/my-llm-trading-bots-beat-the-benchmark-the-rules-i-wrote-beforehand-say-it-doesnt-count-3455)). That discipline is worth copying. These blogs are anecdotal and survivorship-biased, so use them for engineering lessons, not performance claims.

## The "trading firm" pipeline works, but a leaner portfolio loop fits Claude better

The dominant open-source design is **TradingAgents** (about 109k GitHub stars). Fundamentals, sentiment, news and technical analysts run in parallel. Bull and bear researchers then "critically debate analyst insights". A trader sets timing and size, and a risk team plus a portfolio manager "approves/rejects trade proposals before execution" ([TradingAgents GitHub](https://github.com/TauricResearch/TradingAgents)). It is built on LangGraph and splits a cheap `quick_think_llm` from a strong `deep_think_llm`. Claude is one of 15+ supported providers. It keeps a decision log that injects realized returns as "prior lessons" into later runs. Version 0.5.0 (September 2026) added point-in-time data integrity and portfolio-aware runs. Other designs vary the pattern. **ai-hedge-fund** (about 64k stars) uses famous-investor persona agents plus risk and portfolio managers. It "does not actually make any trades", and its backtest mode hides ticker, industry and dates from prompts to reduce memorization ([ai-hedge-fund GitHub](https://github.com/virattt/ai-hedge-fund)). **FinMem** adds layered long-term memory ranked by relevance and recency ([arXiv 2311.13743](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13743)). **FinRobot** is aimed mainly at equity-research reports rather than execution ([FinRobot GitHub](https://github.com/AI4Finance-Foundation/FinRobot)).

The per-ticker debate pipeline has two weaknesses for a personal Claude build. First, it costs roughly 20–40 times more than a lean design (see the cost section). Second, it reasons about each stock in isolation, so correlation, cash and total exposure are afterthoughts, which is why TradingAgents only recently added portfolio awareness. A better fit is a **portfolio-level loop**. Python fetches data and computes indicators. Cheap per-ticker analyst calls return structured JSON. An optional critique pass follows. Then a single strong-model call sees every summary plus the current portfolio and outputs target weights with a rationale. Deterministic code handles everything after that. This design also follows the evidence that architecture matters more than the model. It gives you a clean place to add reflection memory: a decision log plus realized P&L, evaluated against factor-adjusted benchmarks. That matters because one 2026 study found LLM agents' headline returns are "largely explained by passive exposure to market and style factors" ([arXiv 2609.05663](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05663); attribution from a search snippet, unverified). No controlled study has compared debate designs with a single strong model on the same live window. That comparison is worth running yourself in the later phases.

## Claude's Messages API beats the Agent SDK for a scheduled trading loop

Anthropic offers three ways to build this in Python.

**Messages API.** The plain Messages API with tool use and structured outputs lets your code own a fixed sequence of calls. Use `client.messages.parse()` with Pydantic models, or `output_config.format`, or `strict: true` tools, so every decision object validates against a schema. On Claude Opus 5.5 a forced `tool_choice` returns a 400 error, so use structured outputs or `auto` with strict tools instead. Thinking is adaptive with an `effort` setting from `low` to `max`. Opus 5.5 cannot disable thinking and defaults to `medium` effort ([Anthropic migration guide](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/migration-guide); [tool use overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/overview)).

**Claude Agent SDK.** The SDK (`pip install claude-agent-sdk`) is the Claude Code harness as a library. It provides subagents with their own model and effort settings, `PreToolUse`/`PostToolUse` hooks, a `can_use_tool` callback that can return `PermissionResultDeny`, in-process MCP tools declared with `@tool` and `create_sdk_mcp_server`, `output_format` JSON schemas, and a `max_budget_usd` cap ([Agent SDK Python reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-sdk/python)).

**Managed Agents.** Anthropic hosts both the loop and the sandbox, for model rates plus $0.08 per running session-hour ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)).

For a scheduled rebalance, the Messages API is simpler, cheaper and easier to audit. The Agent SDK's built-in Bash, Write and WebFetch tools add token overhead and autonomy you do not want near a broker. The SDK earns its place in a separate, more open-ended research agent, for example a weekly strategy review that reads filings. If you use it, disallow Bash and Write. Expose only your own read-only data tools plus an order-proposal tool routed through the risk engine. Add a `can_use_tool` deny on any order tool when `LIVE=false`, and set `max_budget_usd` for each run.

Mature MCP servers now exist for most of the stack, and they are best used for research, not order placement.

One technical constraint shapes the choice. The Messages API's MCP connector accepts only remote HTTPS servers, while stdio servers such as Alpaca's and EDGAR's work only through the Agent SDK. The TradeTrap results add a security argument: an MCP tool that returns false prices can drive an agent to ruin. In the core loop, call the broker's Python SDK (alpaca-py, or ib_async for IBKR) from deterministic code. Treat MCP as a convenience for prototyping and interactive research.

## Alpaca paper plus EDGAR gives a free, realistic-enough starting stack

**Alpaca** is the easiest broker for a Portuguese- or Brazilian-origin user. New accounts are paper accounts by default ([Alpaca](https://alpaca.markets/learn/live-trading-account-non-us)), and Alpaca serves 195+ countries, including Portugal residents ([Alpaca countries](https://alpaca.markets/support/countries-alpaca-is-available)). Alpaca announced EEA passporting to 29 countries in July 2026, though the regulated entity could not be verified ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260707116782/en/Alpaca-Completes-EEA-Passporting-to-29-Countries-Expanding-Access-to-Regulated-Investment-Services-Across-Europe)). Its free data plan gives real-time equities from **IEX only** at 200 requests/min. Full consolidated (SIP) data costs about $99/month ([Alpaca Data](https://alpaca.markets/data)). Paper fills are optimistic. Alpaca's simulator ignores market impact, latency slippage, queue position and regulatory fees. It does not check order size against quoted depth, and it simulates partial fills at random 10% of the time ([Alpaca Docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/paper-trading)). Your system should subtract a modeled cost of spread plus a fixed number of basis points from paper P&L.

The alternatives each have a catch. **Interactive Brokers** covers EU-listed instruments and more asset classes. Its paper account needs market-data subscriptions shared from the live login, which can take 24 hours ([QuantConnect IB docs](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/cloud-platform/live-trading/brokerages/interactive-brokers)). Most Python users drive it through `ib_async` ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/ib_async)). The **Tradier** sandbox has 15-minute-delayed data ([Tradier FAQ](https://docs.tradier.com/docs/faq)). For crypto, **Bybit Demo Trading** is the most realistic option because it uses mainnet public data and starts with 50,000 USDT ([Bybit docs](https://bybit-exchange.github.io/docs/v5/demo)). The Binance Spot Testnet resets about monthly without notice ([Binance docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/binance-spot-api-docs/testnet/general-info)). The Coinbase sandbox returns static responses ([Coinbase docs](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/coinbase-app/advanced-trade-apis/sandbox)), so use both for integration tests only.

For data, **SEC EDGAR** is the highest-value free source for an LLM agent: filings and XBRL facts at up to 10 requests/second with a descriptive User-Agent ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/accessing-edgar-data)). **Massive** (formerly Polygon) is free at 5 calls/min on delayed data, with paid plans at $29, $79 and $199/month ([Massive KB](https://massive.com/knowledge-base/article/what-is-the-request-limit-for-massives-restful-apis)). The **Alpha Vantage** free tier allows only 25 requests/day ([Alpha Vantage](https://www.alphavantage.co/premium/)). **yfinance** throws undocumented rate-limit errors ([issue #2422](https://github.com/ranaroussi/yfinance/issues/2422)), and IEX Cloud has shut down. Store every bar and filing locally in Parquet or DuckDB. The agent then decides from a reproducible snapshot, which is also your audit trail.

## Guardrails belong in code the model cannot touch

The consensus from the evidence, the builders and the TradeTrap stress tests is a single principle. **The LLM outputs a proposal. It never holds broker credentials, and a deterministic risk gate decides what reaches the broker.** Hard limits live in configuration, not the prompt. Start with maximum position (5–10% of equity), maximum gross exposure, maximum order notional, maximum orders per day, and an allowlist of liquid large caps and ETFs. Start long-only in a cash account, with no margin, shorts, options or leverage. That rules out, by construction, the leverage failure that sank Alpha Arena. Add a daily-loss circuit breaker that halts trading for the day. Add a peak-to-trough drawdown breaker that halts until a human re-enables the agent. Halt as well on stale quotes or repeated API errors. Build a tested kill switch that cancels open orders and disables the loop.

Read account state from the broker at the start of every cycle, never from the model's memory, so corrupted state cannot compound. Sanity-check prices across two sources before acting. Use deterministic `client_order_id`s so retries cannot duplicate orders. Keep an append-only audit log of every prompt, data snapshot, model ID, rationale, proposal, risk verdict and broker response. Send alerts on fills, breaches and errors, plus a daily "heartbeat" message, so a silent bot cannot pretend to be trading.

Two US rule changes matter when you move beyond paper. The SEC approved FINRA's Rule 4210 amendments, which **eliminate the $25,000 pattern-day-trader minimum** effective June 4, 2026. Brokers have until October 20, 2027 to implement the change, and intraday margin monitoring replaces the old rule ([SEC Release 34-105226](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/finra/2026/34-105226.pdf); [Schwab](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/sec-approves-scrapping-25000-day-trader-minimum)). Check which regime your broker enforces during the transition. For US taxpayers, the **wash sale rule** disallows a loss when a substantially identical security is bought within 30 days before or after the sale ([IRS Pub 550](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550)). An agent that keeps re-entering the same tickers triggers it constantly, so log tax lots from day one.

## A daily Claude loop costs about $10–17 a month; hourly crypto costs 30 times more

Anthropic's prices as of September 26, 2026 make model choice a cost lever, not just a quality one ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)).

| Model | Input / Output per MTok | Cache read | Batch (−50%) | Suggested role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Claude Fable 5.1 | $10 / $50 | $0.25 | $5 / $25 | Occasional deep strategy review only |
| Claude Opus 5.5 | $4 / $20 | $0.20 | $2 / $10 | Portfolio decision (effort medium/high) |
| Claude Opus 5 | $5 / $25 | $0.50 | $2.50 / $12.50 | Superseded by the cheaper Opus 5.5 |
| Claude Sonnet 5 | $2 / $10 | $0.20 | $1 / $5 | Bull/bear critique, harder analyst work |
| Claude Haiku 4.5 | $1 / $5 | $0.10 | $0.50 / $2.50 | Per-ticker news/filing summaries |

Sonnet 5's introductory $2/$10 price is now permanent. Claude 4.7 and later models use a tokenizer that produces about 30% more tokens for the same text. Cache writes cost 1.25× input (5-minute TTL) or 2× (1-hour TTL). Batch processing halves both input and output prices and stacks with caching ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)).

The following figures are my estimates, built from assumed token counts, not measurements. Assume a Haiku 4.5 summary per ticker (about 6k tokens in, 0.6k out, ≈ $0.009) and one Opus 5.5 portfolio decision (about 40k in, 8k out including thinking, ≈ $0.32). One cycle then costs about **$0.48 for 20 tickers and $0.81 for 50**. A daily rebalance on 21 trading days comes to **$10–17 per month**, or **$8–13** if the analyst step runs overnight through the Batch API. An hourly loop during US market hours costs about $70–120/month, and hourly 24/7 crypto about $350–580/month. A full TradingAgents-style per-ticker pipeline on Claude costs about **$0.55 per ticker per cycle**. That is about $230–580/month daily for 20–50 tickers, and about $11,900/month for 30 crypto tickers hourly.

Caching helps less than you might expect. A 5-minute cache gives no reuse between hourly cycles. It pays off within a cycle, when 30 analyst calls share one system prompt, and on Opus 5.5's large static policy prefix. Two practical consequences follow. At daily cadence, market-data subscriptions (for example $99/month for SIP) will cost more than Claude does. And cadence is a much bigger cost driver than model choice, which fits the evidence that low turnover is good anyway. Measure real usage with `messages.count_tokens` and `response.usage` during the first paper week before trusting these numbers.

## Trading your own account is legal; managing others' money is not

In all three jurisdictions, an individual running an algorithm on their own account through a retail broker needs no license. In the **EU**, MiFID II Art. 2(1)(d) exempts dealing on own account. The exemption is lost for exchange members, users with direct electronic access, **high-frequency algorithmic traders**, and anyone also executing client orders ([ESMA Single Rulebook](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mifid-ii/article-2-exemptions)). A daily LLM agent is nowhere near high-frequency trading. ESMA's AI statements, including the May 2024 public statement and the February 2026 supervisory briefing on algorithmic trading, address regulated firms, not individuals ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/document/public-statement-ai-and-investment-services); [Macfarlanes](https://www.macfarlanes.com/insights/102mpep/algorithmic-trading-and-artificial-intelligence-esma-supervisory-briefing/)). In the **US**, registration under the Advisers Act is triggered by advice given for compensation. The publisher's exclusion from Lowe v. SEC covers only impersonal, regularly circulated publications, so a paid signals service or personalized alerts is risky ([Justia](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/472/181/)). In **Brazil**, CVM Resolution 19/2021 explicitly covers consultancy delivered through algorithms. It requires registration where investors have little control over a pre-defined strategy. It does not apply to someone who sells a tool that executes the investor's own decisions ([CVM Resolução 19](https://conteudo.cvm.gov.br/legislacao/resolucoes/resol019.html); [CVM investor portal](https://investidor.cvm.gov.br/menu/Menu_Investidor/prestadores_de_servicos/robos_investimento.html)). The bright lines are the same everywhere: do not pool friends' or family money, sell signals, or market the bot as advice without a local lawyer.

Taxes depend on your tax residency, not on where the broker is located, and they reward exactly what the evidence recommends: low turnover. In **Portugal**, securities gains are taxed at a flat 28%. For holdings under 365 days, aggregation into progressive income-tax rates is mandatory for top-bracket earners. Crypto held 365 days or more is exempt, while shorter holdings pay 28% ([CGD](https://www.cgd.pt/Site/Saldo-Positivo/leis-e-impostos/Pages/impostos-investimentos.aspx); [Portal das Finanças](https://info.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/apoio_contribuinte/Folhetos_informativos/Documents/Criptoativos.pdf)). In **Brazil**, swing-trade gains are taxed at 15% with a R$20,000/month sales exemption, and day trades at 20% with no exemption. The proposed flat 17.5% (MP 1.303/2025) was rejected ([InfoMoney](https://www.infomoney.com.br/mercados/day-trade-pode-ter-queda-de-20-para-175-em-aliquota-mas-swing-trade-deve-ter-alta/); [Daycoval](https://blog.daycoval.com.br/mp-1303/)). Brazil's rules for gains held at foreign brokers (Law 14.754/2023) and EU MiCA rules for crypto bots were not verified in this research and need checking before any live phase. Paper trading itself creates no tax or regulatory exposure.

## Recommended architecture: a lean, auditable daily loop in Python

The design below follows every lesson above. It uses low turnover and one portfolio-level decision. Limits, execution and state stay in deterministic code, and every decision can be replayed.

```
 cron / APScheduler (e.g. 15:00 ET on trading days)
        |
 [1] DATA LAYER (Python, no LLM)
     alpaca-py bars + IEX quotes, EDGAR filings/XBRL, news headlines
     -> indicators/factors -> Parquet/DuckDB snapshot (hash stored)
        |
 [2] ANALYSTS (Claude Haiku 4.5, parallel or overnight Batch)
     one call per ticker -> TickerView JSON {thesis, catalysts, risks, score -2..+2}
        |
 [3] (optional, Phase 3) CRITIC (Claude Sonnet 5): bull/bear challenge of top changes
        |
 [4] PORTFOLIO MANAGER (Claude Opus 5.5, effort=high, messages.parse -> Pydantic)
     input: cached policy prefix + all TickerViews + broker-reconciled positions
            + prior decision log with realized P&L
     output: TargetPortfolio {weights{symbol: 0..max}, cash_weight, rationale, confidence}
        |
 [5] RISK ENGINE (pure Python, unit-tested, config-driven)
     allowlist, max 10% per name, max 25% per sector, min 10% cash, turnover cap,
     max orders/day, daily-loss + drawdown breakers, stale-data check -> clip or reject
        |
 [6] EXECUTION (pure Python): diff target vs broker positions -> limit orders,
     deterministic client_order_id, slippage model applied to shadow P&L
        |
 [7] AUDIT + MONITORING: append-only log (prompt, snapshot hash, model id, output,
     verdict, fills), daily Telegram/email report, heartbeat, kill switch
```

The portfolio manager's system prompt should be long, static and cached. It holds the investment policy, the output schema and the reminder that cash is a valid position, which counters Claude's documented long bias. It should also ask the model to name what would make it wrong. Keep the volatile market data after the cached prefix. Run the decision call three times and use the median weights. This damps the run-to-run inconsistency documented in the benchmarks for about $0.64 extra per day. Record `claude-opus-5-5` and the prompt version in every log line, so a model upgrade shows up as a visible break in the series rather than silent drift.

### Phased plan

| Phase | Duration | What you build or run | Exit criteria |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0: Foundations | 1–2 weeks | Alpaca paper account; data layer with local snapshots; risk engine and execution module with unit tests (including the zero-quantity and duplicate-order cases); kill switch; audit log; alerting | Every risk rule has a failing-order test; the kill switch cancels all paper orders in under 1 minute |
| 1: Shadow mode | 2–4 weeks | Full Claude loop runs daily, but orders are only logged, not sent. Write the success criteria down now (pre-registration) | Zero schema failures or unhandled errors for 10 consecutive days; measured token cost within 2× of estimate |
| 2: Autonomous paper | 90 days minimum | Orders go to Alpaca paper autonomously. Benchmarks run in parallel: SPY buy-and-hold, equal-weight buy-and-hold of the universe, and a simple momentum rule | Judged on pre-registered criteria: beat equal-weight buy-and-hold net of modeled 10 bps/trade and LLM cost, with lower max drawdown, plus a market-beta-adjusted comparison |
| 3: Ablations and extensions | 2–3 months | Test one change at a time: reflection memory, Sonnet critic, Sonnet-only vs cascade, weekly vs daily cadence; optionally a separate Bybit Demo crypto sleeve with the same risk engine | Keep only changes that improve risk-adjusted results out of sample; drop the rest to save cost |
| 4: Optional small live | Open-ended | Separate live keys, explicit `LIVE=true` opt-in, capital cap you can afford to lose, human approval above a size threshold for the first weeks, weekly live-vs-paper fill comparison | Live slippage stays within the paper model's assumptions; any breaker trip triggers a review before restart |

The 90-day minimum in Phase 2 follows the practitioner rule above. Even 90 days is one market regime, so read a positive result as permission to keep testing, not as proof of edge. Every Phase 2 result is a forward test on data the model never saw in training, which is the only kind of evidence the literature treats as clean. A result that merely matches buy-and-hold at lower drawdown would already match the best outcomes reported in the benchmarks.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the goal. Autonomy here means building a system trustworthy enough to act without you, not handing Claude the keys. Claude's most useful contributions are synthesis, reading filings and news across a universe, and explaining its reasoning in auditable form. Its weaknesses in leverage, turnover, bias and state trust are exactly the things deterministic code handles well. Designs that let the model trade directly have produced the headline losses. Designs that confine it to proposals have produced survivable, if unspectacular, results.

Two further points follow. First, the costs are lopsided. At daily cadence Claude costs less than a market-data subscription, so spending more on the model (running the ensemble, using higher effort) is cheap, while spending more on turnover is expensive in fees, slippage and, for Portuguese residents, taxes on holdings under 365 days. Second, the most valuable thing this project produces is a clean, pre-registered forward test of a Claude portfolio manager, not a trading edge. No peer-reviewed study has yet published one for Opus 5.x, so your own audit log would be genuinely new evidence, whichever way it comes out.
